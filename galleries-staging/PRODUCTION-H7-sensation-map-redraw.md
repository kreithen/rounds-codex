## What is wrong

The SENSATION MAP on page 5 is a **plantar (sole) view** — the toe pads, arch and heel pad are all
visible. Its legend is *Deep peroneal / Sural / Saphenous / Plantar*.

**No peroneal branch supplies any skin on the sole.** The deep peroneal nerve supplies only the first
dorsal web space; the sole is tibial-nerve territory throughout (medial and lateral plantar nerves,
with medial calcaneal at the heel) apart from thin sural and saphenous border strips.

Region by region, as currently drawn:

| region of the sole | colour drawn | legend meaning | correct? |
|---|---|---|---|
| all toes | green | Saphenous | **wrong** — saphenous never reaches the toes |
| central sole | blue | Sural | **wrong** — sural is a narrow lateral strip only |
| medial ball | purple | Deep peroneal | **anatomically impossible** on a sole |
| medial arch | pink | *not in the legend at all* | **wrong** — medial plantar |
| heel | orange | Plantar | roughly right (medial calcaneal) |

Six colours appear on a four-item legend, so the panel reads as a generic reflexology-style foot
rather than a nerve map built to its own key.

**There is also a view mismatch.** The exam bullets beside the map ask the reader to test the dorsum
of the foot and the first web space. Both are on the **top** of the foot, and a sole view cannot show
either one.

---

## What we need

### 1. Replace the panel with a DORSAL (top-of-foot) view

Territories, which is what the corrected exam bullets teach:

- **Superficial peroneal** — the broad top of the foot and the middle three toes. This nerve is
  missing from the current legend entirely and is the one most of the dorsum belongs to.
- **Deep peroneal** — the **first web space only**, between the great and second toes. A small wedge.
  This is the whole point of the correction: it currently owns a large territory it does not supply.
- **Sural** — the lateral (little-toe) border and the little toe.
- **Saphenous** — the medial (great-toe) border and the great toe.

If a plantar inset is kept alongside the dorsal view, it should be almost entirely the tibial /
"Plantar" colour, with only thin sural (lateral) and saphenous (medial) border strips, **and no
peroneal colour anywhere on it.**

**An approved reference layout is attached** showing exactly these four territories and their
boundaries. Dr. Kreithen has approved it as a *specification of the layout*. It is **not** finished
artwork and is not in the gallery's style — please re-render from source in the house style, using it
only to place the boundaries.

### 2. Add a fifth entry to the legend

`Superficial peroneal`, with its own colour. Keep the existing four.

### 3. Reword the three exam bullets

Currently:

```
- Dorsum of foot (deep peroneal)
- 1st web space
- Plantar foot
```

Should read:

```
- Dorsum of foot (superficial peroneal)
- 1st web space (deep peroneal)
- Plantar foot (tibial)
```

As written the first bullet names the wrong nerve, and the first two are redundant — "1st web space"
*is* the deep peroneal test.

---

## Why this one matters

This is not a labelling nitpick. A clinician who checks sensation over the dorsum of the foot,
finds it intact, and records that the deep peroneal nerve is intact has not tested the nerve they
believe they tested — deep peroneal function would still be unassessed. In a hip-fracture patient the
neurovascular exam is exactly where a missed deficit costs the most.
