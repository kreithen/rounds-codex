# For production: `hyperlipidemia` pages 5 and 6 — figures that cannot show what they are labelled with

Send with the leader-line re-render batch. These are **not** leader-line corrections and are not in
the 81-page work order: on both pages the figure is missing the body part the label names, so there
is no endpoint to move a line to. Both need the figure changed.

Every finding below was checked on the shipped page at 4–8×, not taken from a list.

---

## Page 5, panel 3 — "TARGETED CARDIOVASCULAR EXAM"

**The figure is a heart sitting directly on two lower limbs.** No head, no neck, no abdomen, no arms
— the heart's outflow simply continues into the vasculature of two legs. It then carries a
five-point physical-examination checklist, and four of the five points cannot be performed on it:

| bullet | leader ends on | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Auscultate for murmur of aortic stenosis | the upper heart | acceptable |
| **Assess carotid upstrokes and bruits** | the right border of the heart | **no neck or carotid is drawn** |
| **Evaluate peripheral pulses (dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial)** | mid-thigh | **the ankles and feet ARE drawn, at the bottom of the figure** |
| **Look for abdominal or femoral bruits** | the lower leg | **no abdominal aorta is drawn; femoral is upper thigh** |
| **Inspect for corneal arcus (<45 years)** | the foot | **no head and no eye exist in the figure** |

Two of those four have no correct endpoint at any location. Only the peripheral-pulses bullet is a
simple displacement — the structures it names are drawn, at the ankle, and the line stops at the
thigh.

**What we need:** a figure that carries the exam it is captioned with. Either extend it to a whole
body (head, neck, abdomen, arms) so all five points have a target, or split the checklist so each
bullet sits beside a figure that can show it. If the heart-and-legs figure is kept, the carotid and
corneal-arcus bullets must move off it.

## Page 5, panel 2 — "GENERAL APPEARANCE & VITAL SIGNS"

This figure **does** have a head, which makes the pointers harder to excuse. Four of six are wrong:

| label | leader ends on | should be |
|---|---|---|
| **Central adiposity (common)** | the face, at the cheek/jaw | the abdomen |
| **Hypertension** | the temple / ear | no visible sign exists — drop the leader or point at nothing |
| **Xanthelasma (diagonal folds at medial eyelids)** | the left shoulder | the medial eyelid, which is drawn |
| **the eyelid photograph's own tether** | the mid-upper arm | the eyelid |
| Xanthomas are uncommon except… | the right forearm | prose, not a sign — no pointer needed |
| Xanthomas on extensor surfaces | the left forearm/elbow | **correct** |

*One correction to our own earlier note:* Central adiposity was recorded as ending in blank
background beside the head. It ends **on the face**. The error is the same size; the description was
not exact, and we would rather you have the accurate one.

## Page 6 — "ANKLE–BRACHIAL INDEX (ABI)"

The panel shows **a foot and ankle with a blood-pressure cuff on the calf**, beside a waveform
tracing. There is no arm anywhere in the panel.

- "Ankle SBP 120 mmHg" ends on the waveform. Acceptable.
- **"Brachial SBP 140 mmHg"** doglegs left and ends **on the dorsum of the foot**, on the curved
  cuff tubing that runs across it.

A brachial pressure is measured on the upper arm. As drawn, the page shows both pressures being taken
from the same foot, which is exactly the misunderstanding an ABI figure exists to prevent.

**Worth knowing before you change anything:** the *numbers* are right. 120 / 140 gives the stated ABI
of 0.86, and the caption "ABI < 0.90 indicates peripheral artery disease" is correct. So this is a
figure problem only — please do not adjust the values.

**What we need:** add an arm with a cuff to the panel, and put the brachial label on it. A small
inset is enough; it does not need to be the same scale as the foot.

---

## Why these two pages together

They are the same fault: **a checklist or a ratio written first, and a figure chosen afterwards that
cannot carry it.** A reader who trusts the pointers on page 5 learns to palpate for a corneal arcus
on the foot, and one who trusts page 6 learns that a brachial pressure is an ankle measurement. Both
are the kind of error that survives a skim because the surrounding text is entirely correct.
