Photoshop worklist

54 pages measured · ordered easiest fix first · 11 August 2026

Every correction I have measured, with the file, the pixel to erase, the pixel to draw to, and what is under each. Grouped by how hard the Photoshop job is, not by how bad the error is — so the top of this document is where to start.

Read the two shortcuts in section 2 before you open anything. On about a third of these pages the correct fix is to move the label text, not the leader line — which is a clean cut-and-paste on flat background instead of repainting over illustration. That converts the hardest class of defect into the easiest.

1

Before you open anything

Decide who owns each page — you or production

These are flat JPEGs. Production's fix is to re-render the page from their source scene, which keeps every other pixel intact. Your fix is to repaint the shipped JPEG. If production later re-renders a page you have edited, your work is silently overwritten. So pick a lane per page and tell them: either "we've fixed these ourselves, skip them" or "leave these to you". Doing both is the one outcome that wastes the effort.

My suggestion: take groups A and B yourself (short, self-contained, and they are the ones production is most likely to get wrong a second time), and leave group C and everything in group D to them.

2

Two shortcuts that save most of the work

Shortcut 1 — swap the label text, not the leader

Where two labels have landed on each other's structure, both leaders are already correct in the sense that each ends on a real structure that the other label names. So the fix is to exchange the two pieces of label text and leave every line untouched.

Label text sits on flat dark panel background. Lasso, cut, paste, nudge into place, heal the two vacated rectangles. No painting over illustration at all.

Shortcut 2 — shift a whole label column by one position

Three pages have a column of labels where every label has taken the next one's structure. Rather than moving several leaders, move the text down (or up) one slot in the column and fix only the one label at the end of the chain that has nowhere to go.

3

The erase-and-repaint method

Where you do have to move a line, this is what I learned doing about a dozen of these with scripts. Photoshop's healing brush will do most of it better than my code did, but the choice of donor is the whole job.

What is under the lineHow to erase it
Flat black backgroundAnything works. Spot healing brush, one pass.
Flat or smoothly shaded tissueHealing brush with a donor 20–30 px away, perpendicular to the stroke.
Textured tissue (gyri, trabeculae, folia)Clone stamp, donor parallel to the stroke so the texture direction survives. Sample often; a single donor smears.
Banded or striated tissue (adrenal cortex, muscle, arbor vitae)Clone along the band, not across it. Find the grain by eye first — on addisons p2 the leaders ran parallel to the striations, so the donor had to be perpendicular. The rule is "clone along the texture's grain", and the grain sometimes runs with the stroke.
A strong edge crosses the stroke — an organ boundary, a vessel wall, a bone cortexDo not erase it. Any donor reconstructs one side and destroys the other. I abandoned two fixes for exactly this. Leave those for a re-render.

Erase the whole polyline, not the straight line between its ends. Several of these leaders bend. On pancreatitis p2 I masked a straight line between the endpoints and left a white diagonal across the aorta — the bounding box and the pixel count both looked right, and only a side-by-side caught it.

Check your work at 100%, then at 25%. A repaint that is invisible at 100% often shows as a smudge at thumbnail size, which is where most readers see the page first.
