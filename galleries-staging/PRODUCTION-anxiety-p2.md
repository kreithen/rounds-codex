1 row flagged in the work order. **Confirmed** — and the independent panel sweep found **two more of
the same kind**, which the order did not list. No leader move fixes any of the three; this is an
artwork decision. *Measured on the shipped page (1024×1536), 2026-08-16.*

The page is already at the standard size — no size change needed.

**Panels swept independently:** the KEY BRAIN STRUCTURES circuit figure (all five numbered nodes and

## 1. The base image is a **midsagittal** section, and that is the whole problem

Established from the artwork itself, not assumed: the figure draws the **corpus callosum in full arc**
(x 370–600, y 340–430), the **thalamus as a cut oval** (x 452–565, y 404–446), the **brainstem in
section** and the **cerebellum with its arbor vitae** (x 600–720, y 470–600). Those are midline
structures seen on a cut, so the plane is midsagittal.

The **amygdala**, the **hippocampus** and the **insula** are all lateral to that plane. None of them
can appear on this image, and none of them is drawn.

| badge | label | centre | what is actually at that point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⑤** | **INSULA** | **(531,427)** | **the thalamus** — inside the cut oval, at its upper-posterior part |
| **①** | **AMYGDALA** | **(448,489)** | below and anterior to the thalamus, at **hypothalamic** level |
| **②** | **HIPPOCAMPUS** | **(517,491)** | at the **midbrain / cerebral peduncle**, anterior to the cerebellum |
| ③ | mPFC | (346,388) | the medial frontal cortex, ~70 px anterior to the genu — **correct** |
| ④ | ACC | (432,354) | the cingulate band arcing over the corpus callosum — **correct** |

**Row ⑤ is the order's row and it reproduces exactly** — including the order's own wording, that it
sits at the posterior-superior margin of the thalamus. It is the worst of the three because the
thalamus is a structure this page *does* draw and *does not* label, so a reader is being shown the
thalamus and told it is the insula.

**① and ② are not in the work order.** They are the same fault: a named structure placed on a plane
that cannot contain it. They read as less wrong than ⑤ only because the tissue under them is
unlabelled elsewhere on the page, so there is no competing name for the reader to pick up.

## 2. What we recommend, and why it is not "move the markers"

There is nowhere correct to move ①, ② or ⑤ to. Three options, in the order we would take them:

1. **Add a small lateral or oblique inset** beside the main figure carrying the three off-plane
   structures — amygdala and hippocampus in the temporal lobe, insula in the depth of the lateral
   sulcus. The main figure keeps its circuit arrows and its two correct nodes; the inset carries the
   three that the plane cannot show. This is the smallest change that makes every marker true.
2. **Change the base image** to a see-through lateral view, so all five sit on structures actually
   depicted. Bigger job, and it costs the corpus callosum / brainstem / cerebellum context that
   makes the current figure readable.
3. **Mark the three as projected positions** — a dashed ring or a "(projected)" note in the legend.
   Cheapest, and honest, but it asks the reader to hold a caveat while learning.

**What we would not do is leave ⑤ where it is.** ① and ② sit on tissue the page never names, which is
merely imprecise; ⑤ sits on a drawn, recognisable thalamus, which is teaching a wrong identification.
If only one thing is changed, change ⑤.
