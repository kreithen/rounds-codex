# Tier 4, re-read — and the original triage was wrong about what tier 4 is

*Read on 2026-08-11, at Dr. Kreithen's instruction, after `dementia` p2 turned out to be tier-2
material. All 37 remaining pages' asserted-move rows read in full.*

---

## The finding

**"Tier 4 = near-misses" is wrong for most of the list.**

The original triage defined tiers 1–3 by *reading* rows and then defined tier 4 by **exclusion** —
everything left over. It was never read. Reading it now:

- **Roughly 22 of the 37 pages carry at least one whole-structure error** — a label on a different
  organ, a two-label swap, a marker that does not exist, or a colour-key contradiction.
- **About 8 are genuine near-misses.**
- **Two are tier-1 grade** — a reader acts on them and is wrong.

`dementia` p2 was not an outlier. It was the first one I happened to open.

**What went wrong:** tier 4 was assembled from what tiers 1–3 did not claim, and tiers 1–3 were
selected on the auditors' most vivid row text. A page whose rows are described flatly — "the FIBULA on
both skeletons" — reads as minor next to "a brain marker on the chest wall", and is not.

## Consequences

1. **The working order changes**: `stroke` p1, `labor` p2, `preeclampsia` p2, `gdm` p2 first, then the
   tier-2 groups above, then the nine near-misses.
2. **Tranche 5 should not be called "the near-misses"** — it will carry four tier-1-grade pages.
3. **Two patterns are now frequent enough to be template faults, not page defects:** a legend entry
   whose marker was never drawn (five pages: `gerd`, `meningitis` ×2, `lymphoma`, `gout`), and a
   two-tip or orphan leader (`stroke` p2, `cdiff` p2 ×2, `dementia` p2, `leukemia` p2). Both belong in
   `PRODUCTION-BRIEF-leader-lines.md` as faults **7** and **8**.
4. **Ranking method, for next time:** rank on *what the endpoint is*, never on row count and never on
   how vividly the row is written.
