## Findings stated backwards — a reader would learn the opposite of the published result

### Pediatrics 2026 #6 — The 2026 AAP Immunization Schedule Diverges from the CDC Schedule

**REVERSED — the schedule's defining feature is divergence, not synchronisation**

The schedule is real (policy statement 26 January 2026, Pediatrics 157(3):e2025075754, Red Book Online, 18 diseases, hepatitis B within 24 hours of birth). THE FRAMING IS BACKWARDS ON THE POINT THAT MATTERS MOST. The submission says the AAP 'issued unified guidance', that it 'Reaffirms evidence-based national standards' and that it creates 'a synchronized clinical framework across primary care, obstetrics, and pediatric subspecialties'. In fact the 2026 schedule BROKE with the CDC: AAP covers 18 diseases to the CDC's 11, ending decades of harmonisation, after ACIP moved infant hepatitis B (including the birth dose) and COVID-19 vaccination from universal to shared clinical decision-making. Twelve other national organisations and 28 states have endorsed the AAP schedule over the CDC's. Describing this as synchronised national guidance would leave a resident unaware that two conflicting schedules exist, which is now a routine clinical and consent problem. Rewritten around the divergence, with the hepatitis B birth dose called out because it is the difference with the least recoverable consequence. The submission's co-sponsor list (AAFP, PIDS, ACOG, NAPNAP) is not asserted as I could not confirm it for this edition.

### Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation 2025 #8 — Structured Home Exercise Is Sufficient in Meniscal Tear with Knee Osteoarthritis (TeMPO)

**REVERSED — the same misreading corrected in the orthopaedics list**

Real trial (TeMPO, NEJMoa2503385, 29 October 2025, n=879). THE FINDING WAS STATED BACKWARDS, in the same way as in the orthopaedic surgery submission processed earlier: the PM&R list says the trial demonstrated that 'individual hands-on PT provides modest additional benefits'. It did not — no meaningful additional pain relief came from standard PT, sham PT or adherence texts on top of home exercise, with a 2.5-point difference that was not statistically significant. Corrected, and framed for rehabilitation prescribing rather than surgical decision-making so it complements rather than repeats the orthopaedics entry. CROSS-SPECIALTY DUPLICATION, deliberate: this trial is also live under orthopaedic surgery. The two entries address different decisions — whether to operate versus what to prescribe — on the same basis as the MeVO trials appearing under both emergency medicine and neurology. YEAR: assigned to 2025 to match the publication date; the submission placed it on the 2026 list.

### Plastic Surgery 2026 #3 — Regenerative Adjuncts in Aesthetic Surgery: PRF and Nanofat Are Not Exosomes

**REVERSED — an unapproved, FDA-warned product class was presented as established practice**

THE MOST SERIOUS CORRECTION IN THIS BATCH. The submission groups 'clinical-grade exosomes' with PRF and nanofat, states that their integration 'Establishes formal clinical protocols for combining biologics with traditional surgical operations to enhance graft retention and accelerate flap vascularization', and concludes it 'improves long-term skin quality, texture, and graft viability'. The regulatory reality is the opposite: NO exosome product has FDA approval for aesthetic use, administering unapproved biologic products violates federal law, and the FDA has issued multiple public safety notifications and warning letters covering manufacturers, distributors and administering physicians. Reported adverse events include blindness, tumour formation and infection. Administering such a product with medical claims has been described as outside the standard of care and a licensure risk. The submitted entry would have encouraged a physician into legal and professional jeopardy as well as exposing patients to harm — which is why it has been rewritten as a warning that separates the autologous adjuncts from the unapproved product class rather than merely softened. PRF and nanofat are autologous and point-of-care and remain reasonable; that part of the submission is kept.

### Pediatrics 2026 #9 — Antibiotic Stewardship in Children and the WHO AWaRe Framework

**corrected — the cited study was not found and the target is wrong twice over**

The AWaRe framework and the over-reliance on Watch antibiotics in children are real, and the clinical advice (high-dose amoxicillin or penicillin first-line for otitis media, sinusitis and pneumonia) is sound and standard. TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) THE TARGET IS WRONG IN TWO WAYS. The submission says the work 'setting a benchmark of at least 60% total pediatric antibiotic usage originating from the narrow-spectrum Access category'. The 60% Access figure is a WHO NATIONAL target covering all ages for 2023, not a paediatric-specific benchmark, and the 79th UNGA high-level meeting on AMR has raised it to 70%. A stewardship programme auditing paediatric prescribing against a 60% paediatric target would be measuring against a number that does not exist. (2) The specific study described — 'a major international study published in The Lancet' analysing global paediatric prescribing patterns — was not found. A closely related Lancet-family paper does exist (eClinicalMedicine, 80 national paediatric guidelines against the WHO AWaRe book) and has been substituted, together with the point-prevalence figures that show the actual variation.

### Plastic Surgery 2025 #7 — Lymphaticovenous Anastomosis for Lower Extremity Lymphedema: Outcomes and Postoperative Compression

**NOT FOUND as described — no LVA-PLD trial, real series substituted**

The submitted citation could not be verified. It describes an 'LVA-PLD Trial', a multicentre trial in BMJ Open establishing operative quality metrics such as minimum micro-anastomotic stitch counts paired with early automated pneumatic drainage, and claims it 'standardizes supermicrosurgical quality benchmarks and postoperative compression timing within lymphedema clinical management guidelines'. ## Wrong study or replaced

### Pediatrics 2025 #3 — Omalizumab for Multiple Food Allergies (OUtMATCH Stage 1)

**corrected — 'disease-modifying' is wrong, and the avoidance advice was unsafe**

The trial is real and the results check out: OUtMATCH stage 1, NEJMoa2312382, 180 participants aged 1-55 (177 children/adolescents), 67% versus 7% on the primary endpoint, FDA approval 16 February 2024. FOUR CORRECTIONS. (1) The submission calls omalizumab 'a disease-modifying monotherapy'. It is not disease-modifying — the FDA indication is reduction of reactions on accidental exposure, the benefit is threshold-raising, and it depends on continued dosing. (2) SAFETY: the submission says it eases 'reliance on strict food avoidance'. It does not. Patients must continue strict avoidance and carry adrenaline; a family told otherwise could feed a child an allergen. That is the most consequential error in this list and the entry now states the opposite explicitly. (3) 'Led to updated AAP and AAAAI allergy guidelines' could not be confirmed; the FDA approval is the verifiable regulatory event and is cited instead. (4) Trial name is OUtMATCH, not OUToMATCH; date 2024, not 2025.

### Pediatrics 2026 #5 — Topical Berdazimer Gel 10.3% for Molluscum Contagiosum (B-SIMPLE4)

**corrected — efficacy overstated and the date is wrong by four years**

The trial is real with the numbers confirmed: B-SIMPLE4, 891 patients aged ≥6 months with 3-709 lesions, complete clearance 32.4% versus 19.7% vehicle at week 12 (P<.0001), week 8 19.6% versus 11.6%, week 4 7.2% versus 3.6%, TEAEs 43.0%, 4.1% discontinuation, none serious. TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) EFFICACY: the submission says the gel 'reliably accelerating viral resolution' and achieved 'statistically significant complete clearance... with minimal localized side effects'. Statistically significant is right; reliable is not — two thirds of treated children had not cleared at 12 weeks, the absolute gain over vehicle is about 13 percentage points, and 43% had a treatment-emergent adverse event, which is not minimal. Both corrected, since this is exactly the entry a parent will be quoted. (2) DATE: B-SIMPLE4 was published in 2022 and berdazimer was FDA approved in January 2024 — it is not a 2026 development. The date field states this; the entry is left on the submitted year, as with other out-of-year citations in this project.

### Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation 2026 #3 — Ultrasound-Guided Needle EMG for Nerve Transfer Candidacy in Cervical Spinal Cord Injury

**corrected — wrong operation named, and 'mandatory standard' is not supported**

The work is real: Mayo Clinic uses preoperative ultrasound-guided needle EMG for upper-limb reconstruction planning in cervical spinal cord injury, and the technique paper is in Muscle & Nerve 2026 (Saddler et al., doi 10.1002/mus.70100). TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) WRONG OPERATION. Both submitted lists describe this as assessing candidacy for TENDON TRANSFER. ## Corrected — dates, journals, numbers, strength of claim

### Pediatrics 2025 #1 — AAP/NASPGHAN Clinical Practice Guideline: Diagnosis and Management of Faltering Weight

**corrected — the diagnostic thresholds given were from a different classification system**

The guideline is real and correctly identified: first joint AAP/NASPGHAN CPG, GRADE, panel 2021-2024, Pediatrics 2026;157(4):e2025075764, and it does replace 'failure to thrive' with 'faltering weight' and move from percentiles to z-scores. THE THRESHOLDS ARE WRONG. The submission gives severity bands of mild −1 to −1.9, moderate −2 to −2.9, severe ≤−3. Those are the ASPEN/Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics paediatric MALNUTRITION severity indicators, not this guideline's diagnostic criteria. The guideline's criteria are: weight-for-length or BMI-for-age < −1.65 z; or, under age 2, weight-gain velocity < −2 z; or a decline of ≥1 z-score. A resident applying the submitted bands would use the wrong cut-off in both directions — waiting for −2 before diagnosing, when a 1 z-score decline already qualifies. Corrected, with the one-z-score point moved into the impact field because it is the substantive change and the submission missed it. Date corrected: 2026, not 2025.

### Pediatrics 2025 #4 — Nirsevimab Effectiveness Against RSV ICU Admission and Acute Respiratory Failure

**matched with corrections — supported, with waning added**

Verified and accurate as submitted: 80% against RSV-associated ICU admission and 83% against acute respiratory failure, from a prospective 27-hospital case-control investigation across 24 states, December 2024 to April 2025. IMPORTANT ADDITION: effectiveness fell from 86% at 7-59 days to 66% at 60-183 days after administration. The submission presents a single flat figure, which would have a clinician assume uniform season-long protection; the waning is what determines dosing timing and belongs in the entry. Also added that most infants were previously healthy, since that is what distinguishes this from the palivizumab era, and the operational definition of acute respiratory failure so the 83% is interpretable.

### Pediatrics 2025 #5 — Whole-Body Hypothermia in Preterm Infants 33 to 35 Weeks with Neonatal Encephalopathy

**matched with corrections — the result is stronger than submitted**

Verified: NICHD Neonatal Research Network randomised trial, 19 centres, July 2015 to December 2022, infants 33-35 weeks with moderate or severe HIE cooled at under 6 hours, no reduction in death or disability at 18-22 months. The submission is correct as far as it goes but UNDERSTATES the finding. It says cooling 'did not reduce the primary outcome' and mentions 'potential adverse event risks'. The actual data point towards harm: 35% versus 29% for death or disability, 20% versus 12% for death, and a Bayesian analysis giving 74% probability of increased death or disability and 87% probability of increased death. Those figures have been added, because 'no benefit' and 'probably harmful' support different conversations with a family and different institutional policy. The submission's practical conclusion — restrict cooling to ≥36 weeks and avoid off-label use — is right and is kept.

### Pediatrics 2025 #7 — Fluoride Exposure and Children's IQ Scores: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**matched with corrections — supported, with the contested context restored**

### Pediatrics 2025 #8 — Residual Exacerbations on Mepolizumab in Urban Children with Asthma (MUPPITS-2)

**corrected — the mechanism is not the clean non-type-2 story submitted**

Real work, correctly identified: MUPPITS-2 in The Lancet (2022, 27% relative reduction in severe exacerbations, autumn-predominant) with the nasal transcriptomic analysis in Nature Communications (2025, PMID 40897727). CORRECTION TO MECHANISM. The submission says breakthrough exacerbations 'were driven by non-Type 2 inflammatory pathways (specifically epithelial hypersecretion and viral macrophage responses)'. The published finding is subtler and partly the opposite: mepolizumab dissolved eosinophil gene correlation but CONSERVED and sometimes strengthened correlation among mast-cell genes, type 2 cytokines and epithelial inflammatory genes, and a separate JACI analysis found activated sputum eosinophils still associated with exacerbations. So residual type 2 activity persists rather than being replaced by a purely non-T2 process. The specific pathways named in the submission were not confirmed and are not asserted. The submission's clinical conclusion — keep controller therapy, do not rely on the biologic alone — is right and is kept, with the 27% figure added so the magnitude is visible.
