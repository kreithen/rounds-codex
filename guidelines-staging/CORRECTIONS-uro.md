## Findings stated backwards — a reader would learn the opposite of the published result

### Urology 2025 #7 — CAPTAIN: MRI-Guided TULSA Versus Robotic Radical Prostatectomy

**REVERSED — the submission asserts equal 2-year oncologic control; oncologic data are not yet reported**

The trial is real: CAPTAIN, 212 men treated at 23 centres 2022–2025, organ-confined intermediate-risk Gleason 7 (GG2–3), randomised against robotic radical prostatectomy. The reported results are PERIOPERATIVE and EARLY FUNCTIONAL — less blood loss, same-day discharge, less pain, faster return to activity at one month, no median penile length change versus a 0.65 cm median reduction after prostatectomy — and the sources state explicitly that LONG-TERM ONCOLOGIC EFFICACY DATA REMAIN PENDING. The submission claims 'MRI-guided TULSA achieved EQUAL 2-YEAR ONCOLOGIC CONTROL compared to robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy'. That result has not been reported, and it is the single claim on which a man's decision between ablation and surgery turns. The submission's '(98%)' continence figure also could not be verified and has been removed; the verified statement is that early continence and erectile function measures favoured TULSA. 'Evaluated by AUA/EAU panels to establish TULSA as a primary organ-preserving alternative' is not a guideline position and has been dropped. Added the loss of whole-gland pathology, which is a real cost of any ablative approach and was absent from the submission.

### Urology 2026 #3 — PRIMARY2: PSMA PET-CT Halves Biopsies in Men With Equivocal MRI

**REVERSED — a non-inferiority de-escalation trial was submitted as improving cancer detection**

The trial is real and important: PRIMARY2, Lancet Oncology 2026, 660 men at seven Australian sites, PSA <20, PI-RADS 3 or high-clinical-risk PI-RADS 2, randomised to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET-CT (n=331) versus systematic transperineal biopsy ≥12 cores (n=329). The submission states that 'PSMA PET/CT SIGNIFICANTLY IMPROVED THE DETECTION of clinically significant prostate cancer (ISUP ≥2) while reducing non-essential prostate biopsies'. The first half is backwards. This was a NON-INFERIORITY trial and csPCa detection was NUMERICALLY LOWER in the PET arm — 39/331 (12%) versus 51/329 (16%) — meeting non-inferiority, not superiority. The submission repeats the error in its practical section, claiming PSMA-PET catches 'occult, aggressive lesions that standard MRI misses'; the trial shows no such thing. What it does show, and what the submission understated: biopsy avoided in 49% of the PET arm, and clinically insignificant cancer diagnoses cut from 32% to 14%. Entry rewritten around the de-escalation result, which is the genuine finding and a strong one. Also corrected: the submission dates it to EAU 2026 presentation only; it is published in Lancet Oncology. Added that a negative PRIMARY score means deferred biopsy under surveillance, not discharge.

### Urology 2026 #5 — Extended Versus Limited Pelvic Lymph Node Dissection at Radical Prostatectomy

**REVERSED — biochemical recurrence is precisely the endpoint where no benefit was shown**

The randomised evidence exists but says nearly the opposite of the submission. The submission claims over-10-year data 'revealed that while extended pelvic lymph node dissection showed no benefit in unselected patients, it provided a SIGNIFICANT IMPROVEMENT IN BIOCHEMICAL RECURRENCE-FREE SURVIVAL and delayed secondary systemic therapy in men with ISUP Grade Group 3–5 prostate cancer'. The published results: the 300-patient phase 3 trial (Eur Urol 2020) found extended PLND did NOT reduce biochemical recurrence; the 1,432-patient cluster-randomised trial (Eur Urol 2024 update) again found NO difference in biochemical recurrence, and the benefit that did emerge with longer follow-up was against METASTASIS, particularly distant metastasis. So the endpoint the submission credits with the benefit is the one where both trials were negative, and the endpoint where a benefit was found is not mentioned. The ISUP 3–5 subgroup specificity and the 'delayed secondary systemic therapy' claim could not be verified and have been removed. Entry rewritten with both trials, their differing templates, and the morbidity that has to be weighed. Added that the larger trial cluster-randomised SURGEONS, not patients, which changes how its result should be read.

## Cited study could not be found

### Urology 2025 #6 — PROBE (SWOG S1931): Cytoreductive Nephrectomy in the Immunotherapy Era — Ongoing

**NOT FOUND — the trial is real but has NO RESULTS; the submission reports findings that do not exist**

PROBE / SWOG S1931 is real (NCT04510597, opened 8 March 2021) and its design is as the submission describes, but the trial status as of September 2025 is RECRUITING and no results have been reported. The submission is written in the past tense — 'The randomized Phase III PROBE trial EVALUATED whether upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy... improves survival' — and then states outcomes: that it 'refines NCCN and EAU renal cell carcinoma practice guidelines, ESTABLISHING clear surgical selection criteria' and 'IDENTIFYING specific patient subgroups (such as those with low metastatic burden) who GAIN A SURVIVAL ADVANTAGE from tumor debulking'. None of that has happened. This is the second entry in the 2025 urology list describing results from a trial that has not reported (see WATER IV), and reporting a subgroup survival advantage from an unreported trial is the most consequential form of the error, because it would justify operating on a patient. Entry rewritten as an ongoing trial, with current practice stated from CARMENA and the existing selection criteria so the entry is still useful, and with the design's own implicit lesson — randomise after the systemic therapy response — kept because it is real and it is how the decision is made.

### Urology 2025 #9 — WATER IV: Aquablation Versus Radical Prostatectomy — Trial in Progress

**NOT FOUND — the trial has NO RESULTS and does not measure what the submission claims**

WATER IV is real (NCT06651632, IDE trial, up to 280 patients with Grade Group 1–3 localised prostate cancer randomised to Aquablation or radical prostatectomy, follow-up to 10 years) but enrolment completion is the only milestone reached and COMPLETION IS ANTICIPATED IN JULY 2027 — no results exist. The submission presents it as a completed 'Multi-center Phase III trial evaluating' the comparison and then makes efficacy claims: that it 'prompts guidelines to expand Aquablation indications beyond BPH into targeted focal therapy for localized prostate cancer' and 'DELIVERS rapid, heat-free tissue resection that ELIMINATES localized cancerous lesions while avoiding thermal injury to surrounding erectile nerves and the urinary sphincter'. Two further errors compound it. (1) The trial's PRIMARY ENDPOINTS are urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction — functional, not oncologic — so even on reporting it will not establish that the technique eliminates cancer. (2) The submission calls it 'targeted FOCAL therapy'; the protocol resects the cancer and most of the prostate, so grouping it with focal HIFU or cryotherapy is wrong. Entry rewritten as a trial in progress with the endpoints stated plainly.

### Urology 2025 #10 — Thulium Fiber Laser Versus Holmium:YAG for Renal Stone Lithotripsy

**NOT FOUND for the fluid-protocol half — no PUSH trial exists; the laser half is real**

Two claims were bundled in this entry. (1) The laser comparison is real and the submission's conclusion is supported by the randomised evidence: a prospective RCT (J Urol, PubMed 39918120) and a multicentre randomised trial across four institutions found no discernible difference between SuperPulse thulium fiber laser and pulse-modulated high-power holmium:YAG in stone-free rate, laser efficiency or complications for 5–20 mm renal stones. Added the countervailing meta-analytic finding (OR 1.57 favouring TFL) with the reason the randomised result should govern — the submission presented equivalence as settled without acknowledging the disagreement. (2) The 'PUSH trial' proving 'forced fluid hyper-hydration did not improve stone-free rates' could NOT be found under that or any related name, and no trial of a postoperative forced-hydration protocol and stone-free rate could be located. The submission's practical claim that it 'relieves patients from burdensome post-procedure fluid protocols' therefore rests on nothing, and has been removed rather than shipped — advising against a fluid protocol on the authority of a non-existent trial is a live clinical instruction. Entry retitled to what is verifiable. Added the dusting-versus-basketing point, which affects residual fragments more than the laser choice does.

### Urology 2026 #10 — ctDNA and Urine Tumor DNA in Upper Tract Urothelial Carcinoma

**NOT FOUND as cited — CURATE-UTUC is recruiting and has not reported; rebuilt on published cohort data**

CURATE-UTUC is real (NCT05595408, multicentre prospective longitudinal cohort, RenJi Hospital) but its recruitment status as of December 2025 is RECRUITING and no results have been presented. The submission states that 'Results from the prospective multi-center CURATE-UTUC trial PRESENTED AT EAU 2026 SHOWED that longitudinal monitoring of paired urine tumor DNA and circulating tumor DNA RELIABLY IDENTIFIES minimal residual disease and PREDICTS RECURRENCE following radical nephroureterectomy.' No such presentation or result could be found — this is the third entry across the two urology lists reporting results from a trial that has not reported (with PROBE and WATER IV). Entry rebuilt on what is published: a cohort study reporting baseline ctDNA positivity 69.2% and utDNA 88.9% falling to 46.2% and 21.4% one month postoperatively, with combined detection described as PRELIMINARY predictive biomarkers; and a separate finding that PREOPERATIVE plasma ctDNA predicts muscle-invasive/non-organ-confined disease and is prognostic for progression-free and cancer-specific survival. The preoperative application has been foregrounded because it is better evidenced than the postoperative MRD use the submission described, and because it addresses the specific clinical trap in this disease — that nephroureterectomy removes the renal function cisplatin requires, so neoadjuvant decisions cannot be deferred. The submission's claim that this 'facilitates timely delivery of post-operative adjuvant therapies' is not supported by any reported trial.

## Wrong study or replaced

### Urology 2026 #2 — POTOMAC: Durvalumab Plus BCG in BCG-Naive High-Risk NMIBC

**matched with corrections — hazard ratio exact, forum wrong, and a five-fold toxicity increase was omitted**

Verified: POTOMAC, Lancet 2025 (PubMed 41115436), 1,018 patients randomised 1:1:1, median follow-up 60.7 months, DFS HR 0.68 (0.50–0.93), P=.0154, 32% risk reduction, 67 (20%) versus 98 (29%) DFS events, median DFS not reached in either arm; FDA approved. The submission's HR and 32% figure are exactly right. Corrections. (1) FORUM: the submission says 'Five-year follow-up results presented at AUA 2026'; the final analysis was presented at ESMO 2025 and published in The Lancet. (2) TOXICITY OMITTED: grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events were 21% with durvalumab plus BCG induction and maintenance versus 4% with BCG alone. In a BCG-NAIVE population — most of whom do well on BCG — a five-fold increase in serious treatment-related toxicity for a disease-free survival gain is the central trade-off, and the submission did not mention toxicity at all. Restored. (3) The submission omits the third arm's result: induction-only durvalumab did not carry the benefit, which makes maintenance part of the regimen rather than optional. (4) 'Positions systemic chemo-immunotherapy combination protocols as a potential front-line standard' overstates — this is checkpoint inhibition with BCG, not chemo-immunotherapy, and FDA approval is stated in place of a guideline claim.

## Corrected — dates, journals, numbers, strength of claim

### Urology 2025 #1 — Randomized Trial of Ureteral Stent Omission After Ureteroscopy for Renal Stones

**corrected — trial confirmed, but it is small and no guideline change has occurred**

Verified: multicentre randomised trial presented at AUA 2025 and published in the Canadian Urological Association Journal; stent omission after uncomplicated ureteroscopy for non-obstructing renal stones ≤1.5 cm was safe with no increase in 30-day complications or unplanned healthcare use, and with better quality-of-life and satisfaction measures. The submission's clinical claim is accurate. Two corrections. (1) SIZE: the submission omits that only 74 patients were randomised, which is the fact that determines how far the conclusion travels — restored, with what it can and cannot exclude. (2) The submission states the trial 'PROMPTS MAJOR UPDATES to AUA and EAU Endourology practice guidelines to establish stent omission as a PRIMARY RECOMMENDATION'. No such guideline change could be verified, and a 74-patient trial would not support a primary recommendation; removed. Added the exclusion criteria explicitly — the submission said 'uncomplicated' once and then generalised to 'uncomplicated stone procedures', which would sweep in ureteral stones and obstructed systems that this trial did not study.

### Urology 2025 #2 — PSMAfore: 177Lu-PSMA-617 Before Chemotherapy in mCRPC

**matched with corrections — rPFS accurate, non-significant overall survival omitted**

Verified: PSMAfore, 468 patients, taxane-naive PSMA-positive mCRPC progressing on a second-generation ARPI, median rPFS 9.3 versus 5.6 months, HR 0.41; FDA label expansion 28 March 2025. The submission is accurate in confining its efficacy claim to radiographic progression-free survival, which is more careful than most entries in this review. The correction is an omission: the final overall survival analysis favoured 177Lu-PSMA-617 but was NOT statistically significant, attributed to high crossover. The submission's practical section asserts the approach 'preserv[es] bone marrow function and quality of life' — marrow preservation relative to taxane is a reasonable rationale but was not an established endpoint here, and radioligand therapy has its own marrow and salivary toxicity, so that has been reframed. Added that the comparator was an ARPI switch rather than chemotherapy, which bounds how the 9.3-month figure should be read, and the radiopharmacy/radiation-safety logistics the submission did not mention.

### Urology 2025 #3 — IMvigor011: ctDNA-Guided Adjuvant Atezolizumab After Cystectomy

**matched — verified, one of the accurate submissions in this batch**

Verified in full: IMvigor011, phase 3, 756 patients into post-cystectomy surveillance with serial ctDNA for up to 1 year, 379 ctDNA-positive at any time, 250 evaluable and randomised (atezolizumab n=167), significant improvement in both disease-free and overall survival, 36% lower recurrence risk, low recurrence risk in persistently ctDNA-negative patients, grade 3–4 AEs 28% versus 22%; presented ESMO 2025 (LBA8) and published in the New England Journal of Medicine. The submission's description — including that ctDNA-positive patients derived a major overall survival benefit and ctDNA-negative patients maintained high disease-free survival without adjuvant therapy — matches the published trial. Additions rather than corrections: the exact figures, the grade 3–4 toxicity rate, and the point that the ctDNA-negative arm was NOT randomised, so 'eliminates overtreatment' (the submission's phrasing) rests on within-trial observation rather than randomised comparison. Also made explicit that serial testing over a year, not a single postoperative sample, is what was validated.

### Urology 2025 #4 — SunRISe-1: TAR-200 Intravesical Gemcitabine System in BCG-Unresponsive NMIBC

**matched with corrections — response rate verified, trial phase and guideline claim adjusted**

Verified: SunRISe-1, published in the Journal of Clinical Oncology, cohort 2 of 85 patients with BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC with CIS, complete response 82.4% (reported as 82.8% in some coverage), median time to response 2.8 months, median duration of response 25.8 months, 51% maintaining CR at ≥1 year, FDA approved. The submission's 'exceeding 80%' is right. Corrections. (1) PHASE: the submission calls it 'registration trial data' for 'SunRISe-1 / SunRISe-5'; SunRISe-1 is a PHASE 2B SINGLE-ARM study, and SunRISe-5 is a separate trial in a different population — conflating them overstates the evidence, so the entry is confined to SunRISe-1. (2) The submission says it 'updates AUA and EAU NMIBC guidelines to position device-assisted intravesical delivery systems as a primary bladder-preserving alternative to radical cystectomy'; FDA approval is verified but the guideline repositioning is not, so the approval is stated instead. Added the durability figures the submission omitted and the substantive caveat it skipped: no randomised comparison against cystectomy exists, and progression to muscle-invasive disease during bladder preservation is the irreversible outcome that has to be planned for.

### Urology 2025 #5 — BOND-003: Intravesical Cretostimogene Grenadenorepvec in BCG-Unresponsive NMIBC

**matched — verified, with durability figures added**

Verified: BOND-003, phase 3 single-arm, 112 patients with high-risk BCG-unresponsive NMIBC with CIS, complete response 75.5% at any time — the submission's figure is exactly right. Added from the source and missing from the submission: 24-month CR 41.8%, 90% of 12-month responders maintaining response, cystectomy-free rate 84.5% at 24 months, no grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events, and the Rb-E2F selectivity mechanism with the GM-CSF transgene. The 41.8% at 24 months matters because the submission quoted only the any-time 75.5%, which reads as sustained control. Two adjustments: the submission describes it as 'Final results' — the located data are updated results including 24-month follow-up, so 'final' is not asserted; and 'Integrates intravesical oncolytic virus immunotherapy into AUA and EAU guidelines' could not be verified and has been replaced with the factual state (phase 3 data available, bladder-sparing option). The submission's 'induces systemic antitumor immunity' is retained since the mechanism is documented, but is described as the mechanism rather than a measured clinical effect.

### Urology 2025 #8 — EMBARK and PRESTO: ARSI Plus ADT in High-Risk Biochemical Recurrence

**matched with corrections — EMBARK verified, but the apalutamide claim is a different endpoint**

Verified: EMBARK, NEJM 2025 (NEJMoa2510310), presented at ESMO 2025 — 8-year overall survival 78.9% versus 69.5% for enzalutamide plus leuprolide against leuprolide alone, HR 0.597 (0.444–0.804), P=.0006; enzalutamide monotherapy HR 0.83, not significant; entry criteria of PSA doubling time under nine months and non-metastatic disease on conventional imaging; treatment suspension if PSA fell below 0.2. PRESTO's final results support ADT plus apalutamide for metastasis-free survival and delayed castration resistance. The correction is precision, not direction: the submission writes 'finite-duration Enzalutamide (OR APALUTAMIDE) combined with ADT significantly extends SURVIVAL', attributing an overall survival benefit to both drugs. Only EMBARK demonstrated overall survival; PRESTO's endpoint is metastasis-free survival. Also corrected: 'post-surgery or radiation' is right but the submission omits that enzalutamide MONOTHERAPY failed to reach significance, which is what makes the doublet — not the drug — the standard. Added the PSMA-PET tension, since a patient staged with PSMA-PET no longer matches the trial's conventional-imaging entry criteria and clinicians face that mismatch daily.

### Urology 2026 #1 — AUA 2026 Guideline on BPH/LUTS: Expanded Procedural Options

**matched with corrections — every substantive change verified, one count dropped**

Verified against the published guideline (J Urol 2026, 10.1097/JU.0000000000005099, Part III): PAE upgraded from Grade C to Grade B for prostates ≥50 cc including anticoagulated patients at higher bleeding risk; Aquablation evidence extended to the 80–150 mL range; intraprostatic drug-coated balloon offerable for 20–80 cc with prostatic urethral length 32–55 mm and no obstructing median lobe; three-part structure. EAU independently upgraded Aquablation to a strong recommendation in March 2026. The submission was accurate on the substance — PAE to Grade B, Aquablation to 150 mL up from 80 mL, incorporation of drug-coated balloons, and the three-part format. Corrections: the '62 recommendations' count could not be verified and has been dropped; the DCB anatomical criteria have been ADDED, since the submission mentioned the device without its indication, and those criteria are the entire clinical content of a device recommendation; and the submission's claim about 'endorsing dual combination medical therapies (such as tadalafil with alpha-blockers or 5-ARIs)' could not be verified in Part III (which covers procedural/surgical management) and has been left out rather than asserted from the wrong part of the guideline.

### Urology 2026 #4 — Ten-Year Outcomes of Focal HIFU and Cryotherapy: HEAT and ICE Registries

**matched with corrections — figures exact, but a registry was framed as establishing equivalence**

Verified precisely: European Urology, published 11 July 2026, 3,477 patients (2,897 focal HIFU, 580 cryotherapy), 14 UK centres, 2004–2024; ten-year prostate cancer-specific mortality 0.13% and metastases 3.3%. The submission's '99.87% prostate cancer-specific survival (mortality 0.13%)' and '10-year metastasis-free rate of 96.7%' are both exactly right — they are the same published figures stated as complements. Corrections are about interpretation. (1) The submission says the data support recognition of focal therapy 'as a primary organ-preserving treatment option ALONGSIDE radical prostatectomy and radiation' and 'VALIDATES sub-total organ ablation as a SAFE ALTERNATIVE'. This is a registry with NO COMPARATOR ARM; it cannot establish equivalence to radical treatment, and 0.13% cancer-specific mortality against 12% all-cause mortality at ten years indicates strong competing-risk and selection effects that a single-arm cohort cannot separate from treatment effect. Both figures are now stated together so the reader can see it. (2) The submission omits the risk composition — 25% of this cohort had HIGH-RISK disease by EAU criteria, which strengthens rather than weakens the result and belongs in the entry. (3) Added the prerequisites (MRI-visible index lesion, systematic mapping, surveillance with re-biopsy), because the outcomes came from centres applying them and the entry as submitted implied the technique alone produces the result.

### Urology 2026 #6 — LEGEND: Detalimogene Voraplasmid (EG-70) Non-Viral Gene Therapy in NMIBC

**matched with corrections — trial real, phase misstated, and the durability drop-off was omitted**

Verified: LEGEND (NCT04752722), detalimogene voraplasmid / EG-70, non-integrating non-viral intravesical gene therapy expressing IL-12 and RIG-I regulators; phase 1 overall CR 73%; phase 2 cohort 1 preliminary CR 71% (15/21), 67% at 3 months, 47% (8/17) at 6 months; commonest TRAEs dysuria, bladder spasm, frequency, fatigue. The submission's mechanism description is accurate. Corrections. (1) PHASE: the submission calls it the 'Phase II LEGEND Trial'; it is a phase 1/2 study. (2) FORUM: 'presented during the opening plenary at AUA 2026' could not be verified; the documented presentations are ASCO GU 2025 and AUA 2024. (3) DURABILITY: the submission says only that EG-70 'achieved high complete response rates'. The published sequence is 71% at any time falling to 47% at six months on 17 evaluable patients, and in an indication where the alternative is cystectomy, the six-month figure is the one that matters. Restored, along with the comparison to TAR-200 and cretostimogene, which have far more mature data in the same population — the submission presented EG-70 as an established 'key bladder-preserving class' in AUA and EAU guidelines, which it is not.

### Urology 2026 #7 — Break Wave Lithotripsy: FDA-Cleared Anesthesia-Free Stone Fragmentation

**matched with corrections — clearance and trial verified, mechanism and stone size clarified**

Verified: SonoMotion Break Wave, FDA 510(k) clearance 21 January 2026; S.O.U.N.D. pivotal trial at 10 US and Canadian sites, 64 patients, ureteral or renal stones 4–10 mm, 30-minute sessions with no anaesthesia or sedation, 94% completed in clinic, all pivotal non-inferiority endpoints met, 97% would choose it again. The submission's core claims — FDA-cleared, no general anaesthesia, no sedation, no post-procedural stenting, office-based — are accurate. Corrections. (1) MECHANISM: the submission calls it 'focused SHOCKWAVE technology'; it is LOW-PRESSURE FOCUSED ULTRASOUND (burst wave lithotripsy) creating standing stress waves — a materially different mechanism from shock wave lithotripsy, and the distinction determines the acoustic-window constraint. (2) SIZE: 'small-to-moderate ureteral and renal calculi' is replaced with the cleared 4–10 mm range, since that is what bounds the indication. (3) The submission claims it 'prompts revisions in endourology practice guidelines'; no guideline revision could be verified, and FDA clearance is stated instead. Added the 64-patient non-inferiority design and the acoustic-window/operator-skill constraints the submission did not mention.

### Urology 2026 #8 — ZIRCON: 89Zr-Girentuximab PET-CT for Indeterminate Renal Masses

**matched with corrections — trial verified, but the omitted negative predictive value undercuts the claimed use**

Verified: ZIRCON, Lancet Oncology 2024, prospective open-label multicentre phase 3, 36 sites across nine countries, indeterminate cT1 renal masses ≤7 cm scheduled for nephrectomy, sensitivity 85.5%, specificity 87.0%, PPV 92.9%, NPV 75.2%, accuracy 86.0%, no safety signals. Corrections. (1) The submission names it 'ZIRCON-X' and dates the evidence to EAU 2026; the pivotal trial is ZIRCON (2024), and 'ZIRCON-X' as a trial name could not be verified — real-world experience presented at EAU 2026 is plausible but is not the source of the performance data. (2) The clinically decisive omission: the submission says the test lets urologists 'differentiate BENIGN renal lesions (like oncocytomas) from aggressive ccRCC non-invasively' and would 'reduce diagnostic renal mass biopsies and unnecessary PARTIAL NEPHRECTOMIES for benign lesions'. That is a rule-OUT use, and the NPV is only 75.2% — about one in four patients with a negative scan still has clear cell carcinoma. The test's strength is rule-IN (PPV 92.9%), and the entry has been rewritten around that asymmetry, since acting on a negative scan is where a patient would be harmed. Added the five-day injection-to-imaging interval and the cT1 ≤7 cm population limit.

### Urology 2026 #9 — EAU 2026 Guidelines on Urological Infections: Fungal UTI and Non-Antibiotic Cystitis Therapy

**matched with corrections — all three substantive changes verified, scope narrowed**

Verified against the 2026 EAU Urological Infections guidelines and their summary of changes: a new Section 3.17 on diagnosis and treatment of fungal urinary tract infection; updated evidence and recommendations on periprocedural antibiotic prophylaxis for prostate biopsy in Section 3.18.3 with growing support for transperineal biopsy often without prophylaxis; and BNO 1045 (Centaurii herba, Levistici radix, Rosmarini folium) non-inferior to fosfomycin trometamol in acute lower cystitis, with 83.5% versus 89.8% requiring no additional antibiotics between days 1 and 38. The submission was accurate on all three, including naming BNO 1045 correctly — notable, since an obscure-sounding herbal product code is exactly the kind of detail this review has wrongly doubted before. Corrections: the submission describes a wholesale release that 'establishes international standards' for three domains; the 2026 document is a LIMITED update adding 36 studies across two sections, and that has been stated. Added the actual non-inferiority figures — the submission gave none, and 'non-antibiotic treatment works' reads differently against 83.5% versus 89.8% — plus the exclusions (pyelonephritis, complicated UTI, pregnancy) and the point that candiduria is usually colonisation.
