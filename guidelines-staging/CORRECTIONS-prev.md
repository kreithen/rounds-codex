## Citation not independently verified

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #8 — Opportunistic AI Quantification of Coronary Artery Calcium on Routine Chest CT

**citation not independently verified — direction supported, specifics not confirmed**

The technical direction is real and well established in the imaging literature: deep learning can quantify coronary artery calcium from non-gated routine chest CT, and opportunistic screening from lung cancer screening scans is an active field. I did NOT verify the specific multicentre validation studies the submission attributes to The Lancet Digital Health and Radiology, and the submitted hyperlink is a journal home page rather than a paper — which is itself a signal that the citation was not pinned down. I also could not confirm the claim that this 'supported updated preventive cardiology consensus guidelines advocating for opportunistic screening'; the entry says so explicitly rather than asserting a guideline that may not exist. Two caveats added that the submission omits and that determine whether this is useful: non-gated scores are not interchangeable with dedicated gated CAC scoring, and an incidental score with no routing pathway changes no prescribing. Flagged for the physician to confirm the primary citations before this ships as authoritative.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #9 — CONSORT 2025: Updated Reporting Guideline for Randomised Trials

**citation not independently verified — the statement is real, its contents not confirmed item by item**

CONSORT 2025 exists and was co-published across major journals including The BMJ, JAMA and The Lancet, and the submission's framing of it as the first comprehensive update since CONSORT 2010 is consistent with that 15-year gap. I did NOT verify the specific new content the submission attributes to it — 'strict reporting criteria for AI-driven interventions, digital biomarkers, patient-reported outcomes, and health equity metrics', nor that it 'mandates transparent reporting on digital therapeutics, remote monitoring algorithms, and diverse demographic representation'. Those are plausible for a 2025 revision but are asserted here in general terms only. The submission's 'mandates' is also too strong for a reporting guideline, which journals adopt rather than enforce as regulation; reworded. Flagged for confirmation against the published checklist before any specific item is quoted.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #10 — Early Initiation of SGLT2 Inhibitors for Cardiorenal Protection in Chronic Kidney Disease

**citation not independently verified — direction well supported, cited sources not confirmed**

The clinical direction is well established: SGLT2 inhibitors reduce kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events in chronic kidney disease irrespective of diabetes status, and both KDIGO and ADA guidance support albuminuria screening and early initiation. But I did NOT verify the specific 'pooled clinical trial analyses published in The Lancet' the submission cites, and the submitted hyperlink is The Lancet's home page rather than a paper — the same signal as the AI calcium entry. 'Mandate' is also too strong for guideline recommendations and has been reworded. The entry is written around what is genuinely actionable and verifiable in primary care — annual uACR, treating albuminuria as an indication rather than an observation — rather than around a named analysis I could not pin down. Flagged for the physician to supply or confirm the primary citation.

## Corrected — dates, journals, numbers, strength of claim

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #1 — 2025 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation and Management of High Blood Pressure in Adults

**matched with corrections — two claims not confirmed, renal denervation qualified**

The guideline is real (August 2025) and three submitted points check out exactly: PREVENT replacing the Pooled Cohort Equations with renal function and social drivers of health added, primary aldosteronism screening expanded to all stage 2 or resistant hypertension, and renal denervation newly included. THREE CORRECTIONS. (1) Renal denervation is a QUALIFIED option — selected patients, eGFR ≥40, uncontrolled on optimal therapy or intolerant, with multidisciplinary team guidance; 'formally incorporates renal denervation for resistant hypertension' reads far broader. (2) NOT CONFIRMED: the submission says the guideline 'actively encourag[es] <120/80 mmHg for most adults'. Reporting on the guideline states the definitions and stage thresholds are unchanged from 2017, and I could not confirm a <120/80 target recommendation, so it is not asserted. (3) NOT CONFIRMED: the claim that it 'establishes firm blood pressure targets to prevent cognitive impairment/dementia' and 'formally recommends target blood pressure reduction specifically to lower the risk of mild cognitive impairment and vascular dementia'. Plausible given SPRINT MIND, but I could not verify it as a recommendation in this guideline. Also softened 'routine out-of-office blood pressure monitoring is mandated' — guidelines recommend rather than mandate.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #2 — Tirzepatide for Obesity Treatment and Diabetes Prevention (SURMOUNT-1, 176 Weeks)

**matched with corrections — figures confirmed, estimand and date clarified**

Verified: NEJMoa2410819, PMID 39536238, 2,539 participants with 1,032 prediabetic, 176 weeks plus a 17-week off-treatment period. The 94% risk reduction and the 98.7% diabetes-free figure are both accurate (1.3% versus 13.3% onset). TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) The submission's 'sustained weight reductions up to 22.9%' is the efficacy estimand for the 15-mg dose; the mean percent change reported at 176 weeks was −19.7% at 15 mg (−12.3% at 5 mg, −18.7% at 10 mg) versus −1.3% on placebo. Citing only 22.9% presents the most favourable analysis without saying so, so both are given. (2) Date: published 13 November 2024, not 2025. Added the 17-week off-treatment period, since the submission frames this as arresting metabolic deterioration without noting that the benefit is tied to continued treatment.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #3 — Lepodisiran, a Long-Duration siRNA Targeting Lipoprotein(a) (ALPACA)

**matched with corrections — durability figure was overstated**

Verified: phase 2 ALPACA, presented at ACC 2025 with simultaneous NEJM publication, single subcutaneous dose, 93.9% mean Lp(a) reduction over days 60-180 at 400 mg. ONE CORRECTION: the submission says levels were lowered 'by over 94% for up to a full year'. The 93.9% figure is the day 60-180 average; at one year the reduction is approximately 90%, and at 18 months approximately 75%. The submitted phrasing merges the peak effect with the durability window. Corrected, with the 18-month figure added because it is what supports the annual-dosing case the submission makes. Also stated explicitly that phase 2 shows biomarker lowering, not event reduction — the submission's 'eliminate residual genetic risk' presumes the outcome trial has already succeeded.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #4 — ACIP Lowers Universal Pneumococcal Vaccination to Age 50

**matched with corrections — one vaccine option omitted**

Verified: ACIP voted 14:1 to lower the age-based recommendation from 65 to 50 and the CDC endorsed it; the rationale is disease burden in the 50-64 group. TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) The submission lists only 'single-dose PCV20 or PCV21 (Capvaxive)'. PCV15 also remains an option for this age group, with PPSV23 one year later — omitting it would have a clinician turn away a patient offered PCV15, or miss the follow-up dose. (2) Date: the ACIP vote was October 2024, with MMWR publication following; the submission dates it to 2025. ADDED: the serotype coverage difference (about 84% of invasive disease for PCV21 versus about 52% for PCV20 in adults 50+), which is the practical basis for choosing between them and is absent from the submission.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #5 — USPSTF Recommendation: Screening for Breast Cancer Starting at Age 40

**matched with corrections — figure and date, and the conflict is not fully resolved**

Verified: biennial mammography ages 40-74, Grade B, published in JAMA 11 June 2024 following the 30 April 2024 announcement, PMID 38687503. THREE CORRECTIONS. (1) FIGURE: the estimate is 19% more lives saved, not the 20% submitted. (2) Date: 2024, not 2025. (3) The submission says the update 'resolv[es] longstanding policy conflicts between preventive task forces and specialty oncology societies'. It aligned the starting age, but the interval remains contested — several societies recommend annual rather than biennial screening — so the conflict is narrowed rather than resolved. ADDED: the 40% higher breast cancer mortality in Black women, which the Task Force cites as a reason for the change and which the submission omits entirely, and the insufficient-evidence statement on supplemental imaging for dense breasts.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #6 — Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes (SELECT)

**matched with corrections — one figure not confirmed, two major findings added**

Verified: SELECT, NEJMoa2307563, PMID 37952131, 17,604 participants (8,803 semaglutide / 8,801 placebo), 804 sites in 41 countries, mean follow-up 39.8 months, 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with BMI ≥27 and established cardiovascular disease without diabetes. THREE CORRECTIONS. (1) NOT CONFIRMED: the submission claims 'heart failure outcomes by 18%'. I could not verify an 18% heart failure figure and it is not asserted; the confirmed secondary findings are given instead. (2) Date: November 2023, not 2025. (3) The submission states that ACC/AHA and ADA guidelines were updated to recommend GLP-1 receptor agonists in non-diabetic secondary prevention on the basis of this trial; I did not verify those guideline updates, so the entry describes the trial evidence rather than asserting guideline text. ADDED: the 73% reduction in incident diabetes and the roughly 22% improvement in renal outcomes, both absent from the submission and both larger effects than the primary endpoint.

### Preventive Medicine 2025 #7 — Oral Muvalaplin for Lowering Lipoprotein(a) (KRAKEN)

**matched with corrections — the headline figure depends on which assay is used**

Verified: phase 2 KRAKEN trial published in JAMA, muvalaplin as the first oral small-molecule Lp(a) disruptor, 12-week primary endpoint. ONE SUBSTANTIVE CORRECTION: the submission cites a single figure, 'reduced circulating Lp(a) levels by up to 85%', without saying which assay produced it. The trial reported placebo-adjusted reductions of up to 85.8% by intact Lp(a) particle assay but up to 70.0% by conventional apo(a) assay — a 15-point difference driven by assay method, since apo(a) assays also detect fragments. Reporting only the higher number would overstate what a clinician's own laboratory would measure. Both are now given with the reason they differ. Date corrected to 2024, and the phase-2 status made explicit.
