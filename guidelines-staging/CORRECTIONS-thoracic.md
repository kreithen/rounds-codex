## Findings stated backwards — a reader would learn the opposite of the published result

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #5 — Segmentectomy Versus Lobectomy in 2–3 cm (T1c) NSCLC

**REVERSED on the stratification claim — LVI does not make lobectomy mandatory**

The study is real (CHEST 2025, S0012-3692(25)00812-8) and its headline finding — segmentectomy non-inferior to lobectomy in T1c, five-year survival 74.1% vs 74.0% in pathological T1cN0M0 — is confirmed. The submission's clinical rule is not. It states that segmentectomy achieves equivalent recurrence-free survival 'in T1c tumors LACKING VPI/LVI, while lobectomy remains MANDATORY if microvascular/pleural invasion is present'. The published propensity-matched analysis found NO significant difference between segmentectomy and lobectomy in patients WITH lymphovascular invasion — the opposite of a mandate. No source supporting a VPI/LVI-conditional rule could be found, and it is the kind of invented decision rule that reads authoritative and changes an operation. Also corrected: 'propensity-matched trial data' — propensity matching is what you do in the ABSENCE of a trial, and calling it trial data hides the confounding that matters most here; and the submission omits that the randomised evidence for sublobar resection stops at 2 cm, which is the single most important caveat for a 2–3 cm tumour.

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #6 — Postoperative ctDNA as a Prognostic Marker After NSCLC Resection

**corrected — cohort size unverified, and guideline integration REVERSED**

The prognostic evidence is real and has been retained with actual figures (HR 6.41 for RFS on immediate postoperative detection; 13 studies/1,309 patients and 7 studies/1,138 patients). Three corrections. (1) The submission's '3,280+ resected NSCLC patients' could not be verified; the locatable meta-analyses are roughly a third of that, and the figure has been replaced with what is sourced. (2) The submission states this 'integrates post-resection ctDNA clearance as a high-confidence prognostic biomarker in NCCN and ASCO POST-SURGICAL SURVEILLANCE PROTOCOLS'. Neither society recommends ctDNA for routine post-resection surveillance in NSCLC; that sentence is removed and the actual position stated. (3) The submission says the result guides 'the escalation or de-escalation of adjuvant chemotherapy'. No randomised trial has shown ctDNA-guided adjuvant decisions improve NSCLC outcomes — and de-escalation on a negative ctDNA means withholding therapy with a proven stage-based survival benefit, which is the direction with real potential for harm. Restated explicitly. Retained from the submission and verified: lead time over radiological relapse, and the tumour-informed versus tumour-agnostic distinction.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #7 — Diaphragm Dysfunction After Congenital Heart Surgery: Epidemiology and Outcomes

**REVERSED on benefit — a descriptive epidemiology study was presented as proving early plication works**

The study is real and its figures check out: 77,383 index operations, 1,130 (1.5%) with diaphragm dysfunction, 510 (45%) plicated, median diagnosis POD 7, median plication POD 14, worse ventilation/complications/length of stay with dysfunction, no excess major complications from plication itself. But the submission's conclusions do not follow from it. (1) It is described as 'a 77,000+-patient TRIAL' — it is an epidemiology and outcomes analysis. (2) The submission says it 'defines standardized criteria for early diaphragmatic plication'; the paper describes current practice, it does not set criteria. (3) The submission claims plication 'DRAMATICALLY DECREASES ventilator days, pediatric ICU stay lengths, and tracheostomy rates'. That comparison was not made here, and separate published evidence points the other way: no significant ventilation-time difference between aggressive early plication and conservative management (6 vs 8 days, P=0.36), similar functional recovery in plicated and non-plicated patients (60% vs 54.8%), and plicated diaphragm excursion at 77% of the contralateral side. Since the entry as submitted would push a surgeon toward operating on an infant on the belief that a large study proved benefit, the actual evidence has been stated in full. The genuinely reassuring finding — plication did not add major complications — is retained. Note the submission's typo 'unable to weed from mechanical ventilation'.

## Cited study could not be found

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #4 — Resection Without Preoperative Tissue Diagnosis in Indeterminate Nodules

**NOT FOUND as cited — no STS 2026 guideline on this exists; rebuilt from the real evidence**

The submission attributes this to 'STS 2026 guidelines' that 'established clear benchmarks permitting surgical resection without a preoperative tissue biopsy in select high-risk indeterminate pulmonary nodules', with a link to a generic STS topic page. No such 2026 guideline could be found. The underlying clinical position IS well established but comes from surgical series and pulmonary nodule guidance, not a new document: pretest probability above roughly 65% supports resection, ideally thoracoscopic wedge; benign rates in resection-without-diagnosis series are 14.2% (500 patients) and 6% in an upfront-lobectomy subset. Entry rebuilt on those sources. The submission's own instinct — that high-risk diagnostic manoeuvres in deep lesions and pneumonectomy without tissue should be rejected — is sound and has been kept and sharpened, because the graded rule (wedge with frozen section yes, anatomical resection only after confirmation, pneumonectomy never) is the part that actually protects a patient and the submission stated it only loosely.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #9 — DIPNECH and Multifocal Pulmonary Neuroendocrine Disease: No Guideline Exists

**NOT FOUND — no such series or standard exists, and the submitted framing inverts the treatment of a diffuse disease**

No multi-centre prospective series in JTCVS establishing surgical algorithms for DIPNECH could be found, and the submission's clinical claim runs against the disease. It states such a series 'establishes parenchyma-sparing sleeve resections and anatomical segmentectomies as the DEFINITIVE STANDARD, strongly advising against radical lobectomies for multifocal indolent neuroendocrine lesions'. The literature is explicit that there are NO evidence-based management guidelines for DIPNECH; the published surgical experience is case reports and small series of wedge resections and lobectomy; somatostatin analogues are the mainstay for symptom palliation; and resection is considered for individual larger progressive tumours, not for the hyperplasia. Declaring any resection the 'definitive standard' for a diffuse, bilateral, indolent airway process is the wrong shape of recommendation — it would push a surgeon to operate on a disease that is not resectable and whose dominant symptom is medically treatable. Entry rewritten to state the real position, including the parenchyma-conservation argument, which is the one part of the submission's instinct that is sound and which matters precisely because the disease is multifocal. The submission's 'Clinical Guideline Impacts' framing has been dropped: there is no guideline to impact.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #10 — DOACs Versus Warfarin for Postoperative Atrial Fibrillation After Cardiac Surgery

**NOT FOUND as cited — the named study, the length-of-stay figures and the Class I status could not be verified, and the submitted link points at a different document**

The clinical direction is supported but almost none of the specifics are. (1) No 'comparative study published in The Annals of Thoracic Surgery' evaluating discharge DOAC versus warfarin for POAF with a 'median 7 days vs 9 days' length of stay could be found; that figure is unsourced and has been removed. (2) The submission's LINK is a ResearchGate page for the STS 2026 POAF GUIDELINE — a different document from the one the entry describes, and the same guideline that is entry 1 of this list. A citation pointing at an unrelated paper is a tell this review has seen before. (3) 'Endorsing DOACs as the preferred CLASS I alternative to Warfarin for post-op AF lasting beyond 48 hours' could not be verified as a class assignment. What IS verifiable and has been substituted: a 16,902-patient meta-analysis showing lower bleeding (RR 0.74) and fewer major neurological events with no mortality difference, a second synthesis showing lower stroke risk, and registry evidence that factor Xa inhibitors overtook vitamin K antagonists for POAF from around 2020. Added the question the submission skipped entirely — whether and for how long to anticoagulate transient POAF, which is genuinely unresolved and is the decision that precedes agent choice — plus the renal, mechanical-valve and recent-bleeding constraints.

## Wrong study or replaced

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #4 — Robotic Versus VATS Resection After Neoadjuvant Chemoimmunotherapy

**corrected — the conversion figures are wrong, and no guideline update exists**

The clinical claim is supported but the numbers are not. The submission states conversion of '3.1% vs. 8.9%' for RATS versus VATS; the published comparative figures are 7.5% versus 28.2% (P<0.001), with a meta-analysis OR of 0.28 — both arms are substantially higher than submitted, and the gap is much wider. Corrected. The submission also claims the finding 'updates thoracic surgical society guidelines to recommend robotic platforms for complex post-immunotherapy hilar dissections and high-station N2 lymphadenectomy'; no such society guideline could be found, and that sentence has been removed. 'Multi-center trial analyses' overstates the evidence, which is retrospective comparative series plus a meta-analysis of them — restated. Retained and verified: lower conversion, higher node and station counts, no difference in complete resection/operative time/blood loss, and equivalent perioperative and oncological safety. Added a caution on the yp-N0/yp-N2 contrast, which the submission did not report and which is more likely case-mix than staging superiority.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #3 — STS 2026: Nodal Assessment, Upstaging and Survival in Node-Negative NSCLC

**corrected — upstaging rate and nodal threshold both wrong**

The presentation is real: Seder, J. Maxwell Chamberlain Memorial Paper in General Thoracic Surgery, STS 2026 Annual Meeting, 31 January 2026, STS National Database, 48,779 clinically node-negative NSCLC patients. Three corrections. (1) UPSTAGING: the submission says extended sampling 'alters pathologic staging in up to 14% of cT1-T2' cases; the reported figure is 11.2%. (2) THRESHOLD: the submission specifies '>10 nodes across at least 3 N2 stations'. The 2021 North American standard is three N2 stations plus one N1 node, and this study's actual recommendation is that more than one N1 node be removed — a specific, modest change, not a ten-node count. A fabricated numerical threshold in a quality metric is the kind of detail that gets audited against, so it has been replaced with what was presented. (3) The submission claims the work 'establishes stricter quality benchmarks... PENALIZING non-systematic sampling in quality metrics'; no such penalty exists — this is a presented database analysis, not an adopted metric. Also restated as a conference presentation rather than 'a trial', and added the practical point the submission omitted: stations must be submitted separately for the finding to be usable.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #6 — Extended Arch Versus Hemiarch Replacement in Older Patients With Type A Dissection

**corrected — right conclusion, wrong registry, wrong forum, wrong age cut-off**

The finding of no survival difference is confirmed, but nothing about the attribution was correct. The study is Arima, Sato and Tanaka, Interdisciplinary CardioVascular and Thoracic Surgery, published 10 January 2026, volume 41 issue 1, ivag017 — a retrospective analysis of the SHIZUOKA KOKUHO DATABASE, a Japanese prefecture-wide administrative CLAIMS database, in patients aged ≥60, 774 patients with 167 propensity-matched pairs. The submission describes 'a prospective STS registry study presented at STS 2026' in 'patients over 65'. Every one of those is wrong: not STS, not prospective, not presented at STS 2026, and the age threshold is 60. The submission also claims identical 'reintervention rates', which was not among the verified outcomes, and asserts the finding 'slashes operative hypothermic circulatory arrest times and lowers perioperative stroke' — those come from the separate meta-analysis (23 studies, 4,576 patients), which ALSO found hemiarch carries HIGHER LATE MORTALITY, a counterweight the submission omitted entirely. Both sides are now stated, along with the reason the trade-off resolves differently by age and the limitation that claims data cannot see tear location.

## Corrected — dates, journals, numbers, strength of claim

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #1 — AATS Expert Consensus: Surgical Management of Esophageal and GEJ Cancer

**matched with corrections — document confirmed, recommendation count unverified**

Confirmed: 'The 2025 American Association for Thoracic Surgery (AATS) expert consensus document: Surgical management of esophageal and gastroesophageal junction cancer', JTCVS 2025, volume 170 issue 3, pages 884–901, international multidisciplinary panel, modified Delphi. The submission's description of the scope — early-stage, locally advanced adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, endoscopic resection versus radical esophagectomy, margins, two-field lymphadenectomy, post-EMR surveillance — matches the published abstract closely. Two adjustments: the '23 evidence-based recommendations' figure could not be verified in accessible sources and has been dropped rather than asserted; and the submission calls the recommendations 'evidence-based' without qualification, where the document itself states they rest on modified Delphi consensus and expert opinion — that distinction has been restored because it changes how much weight a reader should give a statement in a grey zone. Also removed the submission's claim that the document 'standardizes surgical pathways', which overstates a consensus statement.

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #2 — CHEST Clinical Practice Guideline: Early-Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**corrected — recommendation strength overstated, and the guideline's key surgical finding was omitted**

The guideline is real: CHEST, published July 2025 (PubMed 40562304). Corrections. (1) The submission says it 'formally establishes minimally invasive surgical resection (VATS or RATS) as the IMPERATIVE standard over open thoracotomy for clinical Stage I NSCLC'. The published recommendation is STRONG but on LOW CERTAINTY evidence for stage I, and for stage II it is only CONDITIONAL — the submission collapses two different strengths into one absolute. (2) The submission omits the guideline's most surgically consequential new statement: equivalent overall survival for peripheral ≤2 cm NSCLC treated by SUBLOBAR resection versus lobectomy. Restored, with the size limit stated, because the adjacent entry in this same list extends sublobar resection to 2–3 cm on weaker evidence. (3) 'Confirms routine adjuvant systemic therapy for resected Stage II disease' is imprecise — the guideline specifies chemotherapy PLUS checkpoint inhibitor, and separately adjuvant targeted therapy for EGFR-mutant stage IB(≥3 cm)/II. (4) The '17 evidence-based recommendations' count could not be verified and has been dropped. Added the systematic nodal sampling requirement, which the guideline states and the submission reduced to 'consistent post-resection pathological staging'.

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #3 — NCCN Lung Cancer Screening v1.2025: No Limit on Years Since Quitting

**matched — verified, with the coverage mismatch added**

Fully verified. NCCN Guidelines Insights: Lung Cancer Screening, Version 1.2025 (JNCCN 23(1)): screening recommended at age ≥50 with ≥20 pack-years and NO limit on years since quitting; the stated rationale is that risk in people who formerly smoked remains substantially elevated with no substantive drop-off after 15 years since quitting. The submission's description is accurate, including the point that the 15-year restriction was the one removed and that the effect is to expand the pool of screen-detected stage I candidates. One addition of my own, not a correction: NCCN and USPSTF now differ on this criterion and US coverage tracks the USPSTF, so eligibility by NCCN does not guarantee a covered scan — a reader acting on this entry needs to know that before telling a patient they qualify.

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #7 — Biomarker Selection for Perioperative Immunotherapy in Resectable NSCLC

**corrected — the reasoning is exclusion, not subgroup analysis, and the PD-L1 claim is overstated**

The clinical bottom line the submission reaches — profile for EGFR/ALK before committing to perioperative immunotherapy — is right, and both trials are real. Two corrections to how it gets there. (1) The submission says 'Phase III trial subgroup ANALYSES CONFIRMED that PD-L1 expression levels and targetable driver mutations (EGFR/ALK) dictate the efficacy of perioperative immune checkpoint blockade.' For EGFR/ALK there was no such subgroup analysis in the defining trial: CheckMate 816 EXCLUDED those patients, and KEYNOTE-671 allowed them without mandating testing. The recommendation rests on absence of evidence plus toxicity concerns, not on a demonstrated lack of benefit — a distinction that matters if a reader is ever asked to justify it. (2) 'maximizing pathological complete response in high-PD-L1 candidates' implies PD-L1 selects who should be treated; the trials showed significant benefit even at low or negative PD-L1, so a low value must not be used to withhold treatment. Restated. Removed the submission's unsourced claim about 'surgical delays' as a documented harm, and its assertion that guidelines now formally 'advise against' perioperative immunotherapy in this group, which could not be verified as written.

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #8 — DECIPHER-NODL: Imaging Plus cfDNA Fragmentomics for Pulmonary Nodules

**matched with corrections — model real and strong, but it is not prospective and not in any guideline**

The study is real and better than its odd name suggests — Lancet Regional Health Western Pacific 2025, deep-learning LDCT radiomics plus a four-feature cfDNA fragmentomics model, stacked ensemble, AUC 0.950 internal and 0.966 external validation, with an additional invasion-prediction model. This is the fifth time in this review that a claim reading as fabricated has turned out to be real; the failure mode remains overstated sourcing, not invented science. Three corrections. (1) The submission calls it a 'prospective AI framework' validated 'across 1,350+ indeterminate pulmonary nodules'; the publication is a model DEVELOPMENT AND VALIDATION study, and the 1,350 figure could not be verified. (2) 'diagnostic accuracy superior to traditional Brock and Mayo clinical risk models' could not be verified from accessible sources — the documented comparison is against the individual imaging and cfDNA models, not against Brock or Mayo — so that claim has been reframed as the comparison the field still needs. (3) The submission says it 'integrates multi-modal AI nodule risk stratification INTO CLINICAL GUIDELINES'; it is in none, and no assay is orderable. Removed the claim that it 'significantly reduces unnecessary diagnostic resections', which is an aspiration of the framework rather than a measured outcome.

### Thoracic Surgery 2025 #9 — RAMIE and Left Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve Nodal Yield

**matched with corrections — direction confirmed, magnitude and guideline status overstated**

The finding is real and reproducible across several meta-analyses: RAMIE yields more total nodes and more left recurrent laryngeal nerve nodes than conventional minimally invasive esophagectomy, and the left RLN station is where the technical advantage lies. Corrections. (1) MAGNITUDE: the submission says RAMIE 'yields SIGNIFICANTLY HIGHER lymph node harvest in the left RLN station', which is true statistically and misleading practically — the published differences are 1.74 versus 1.34 nodes (MD 0.22) and MD 0.60. Stated numerically so the reader can judge. (2) The submission claims this 'establishes RAMIE as a PRIMARY RECOMMENDED SURGICAL MODALITY in ISDE and ESMO practice guidelines for locally advanced esophageal carcinoma'. Neither guideline could be verified as making that recommendation; removed. (3) The submission asserts RAMIE 'mitigat[es] postoperative vocal cord paralysis' — the fewer pulmonary complications finding is supported, but a reduction in RLN palsy specifically could not be verified and would be counterintuitive given that the advantage is MORE aggressive dissection at that nerve. Replaced with the practical instruction (deliberate dissection, consider nerve monitoring, assess cord function postoperatively) rather than a reassurance.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #1 — STS 2026 Guidelines: Postoperative Atrial Fibrillation After Cardiac Surgery

**matched with corrections — verified, and moved here from the 2025 list where it was submitted twice**

Verified: Annals of Thoracic Surgery 2026, S0003-4975(26)00344-9, co-led by Chatterjee and Schena, STS Workforce on Evidence-Based Surgery, 15 recommendations across preoperative/intraoperative/postoperative domains, with more assertive guidance on amiodarone and posterior pericardiotomy. Two structural corrections. (1) DEDUPLICATION: the SAME document was submitted as entry 7 of the Thoracic Surgery 2025 list ('STS Clinical Practice Guidelines for Postoperative Atrial Fibrillation'). It is a 2026 publication, so it is kept here on 2026 and removed from 2025 — leaving that year with nine entries, per the project rule that a development appears under one year only. (2) SCOPE: both submissions describe it as covering POAF 'following thoracic AND cardiac surgery'. The published title and content are specific to CARDIAC surgery; the thoracic extrapolation has been flagged rather than carried, since post-resection POAF has a different incidence and evidence base. The submission's Class I/IIa attributions (Class I for perioperative amiodarone and cardioversion, Class IIa for posterior pericardiotomy and beta-blockers) could not be individually verified against the published tables and have been stated as emphasis rather than as class assignments.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #2 — 2026 Multisociety Guideline for Acute Pulmonary Embolism

**matched with corrections — guideline and categories verified, journals and specifics adjusted**

Verified: the 2026 AHA/ACC/ACCP/ACEP/CHEST/SCAI/SHM/SIR/SVM/SVN guideline, published in Circulation (CIR.0000000000001415) and JACC, is the first ACC/AHA guideline on acute PE and does introduce 'Acute Pulmonary Embolism Clinical Categories' with five categories A–E plus subcategories, ranging low to high risk. The submission's URL is correct. Corrections. (1) The submission says it was 'published across Circulation, CHEST, and JAMA'; it appears in Circulation and JACC — CHEST is one of the ten writing societies, not a journal of publication, and JAMA is not involved. (2) The submission's specific operational claims — 'fast-track home discharge for low-risk presentations' and 'expediting thoracic surgical embolectomy consultation for category D/E incipient cardiopulmonary collapse' — are directionally plausible and the Category A home-discharge point is verifiable, but the D/E-to-embolectomy mapping could not be confirmed and is now stated as the discussion the categories enable rather than as a guideline instruction. (3) 'Replaces rigid risk scores' overstates it; the categories are a new scheme, and existing scores are not withdrawn.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #5 — Reoperative Surgical Mitral Valve Replacement Versus Transcatheter Valve-in-Valve

**corrected — study design and follow-up horizon both overstated**

The study is real: Annals of Thoracic Surgery, published April 2026 (S0003-4975(26)00250-X), presented at the Thirty-third Annual Meeting of the Asian Society for Cardiovascular and Thoracic Surgery in May 2025, and its title does report a long-term survival advantage for reoperative surgery. Three corrections. (1) DESIGN: the submission calls it 'a large multi-center REGISTRY TRIAL'. It is a RETROSPECTIVE COHORT of 229 patients at TWO quaternary centres — not large, not a registry, not a trial. (2) FOLLOW-UP: the submission cites 'durable 10-year survival'; the published endpoints are five-year cumulative incidence of death, reintervention and heart failure. (3) The submission's claim of 'lower initial perioperative morbidity (transcatheter)' is partly supported — 30-day MVARC outcomes were SIMILAR except for more prolonged ventilation after surgery, which is narrower than a general morbidity advantage. Added the selection-bias caveat, which is decisive in a retrospective comparison of an operation against a catheter procedure, and the point that both technologies evolved across the 2004–2023 study window.

### Thoracic Surgery 2026 #8 — COVID-19-Positive Donor Hearts in Pediatric Transplantation: Three-Year Outcomes

**matched with corrections — figures exact, guideline claim invented**

Verified precisely: 1,978 paediatric transplants 2020–2024 from the Standard Transplant Analysis and Research database, 60 (3.0%) COVID-19-positive donors, three-year survival 71.5% versus 86.3%. The submission's two survival figures are exactly right. Corrections. (1) The submission claims the study 'REWRITES ISHLT and UNOS pediatric organ acceptance guidelines, placing strict restrictions on donor height mismatches and ISCHEMIC TIMES'. No guideline change could be found; the paper's stated conclusion is that transplant teams should exercise caution pending more data. Ischaemic time was not among the identified risk modifiers — HEIGHT MISMATCH was (P=.048) — so one of the two named restrictions is not in the paper at all. (2) The submission asserts the finding protects recipients 'from LATE GRAFT LOSS'; graft failure was explicitly rare with non-significant cumulative risk, so the mortality is not attributable to graft loss on these data. Corrected. Added the timing pattern (no difference to 1.5 years, divergence at 2–3 years), the baseline imbalances that confound it, and the small exposed denominator — all omitted from the submission and all necessary to weigh a decision that is made against waiting-list mortality.
