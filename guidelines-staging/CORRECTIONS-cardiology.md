**corrected**

The original said baxdrostat caused no clinically significant hyperkalemia — elevated potassium occurred in 2.3-3.0%. And it said the trial 'directly influenced updated AHA/ACC hypertension guidelines, adding aldosterone synthase inhibition as a novel class recommendation'; the drug was not FDA-approved until May 2026, so it could not have been a class recommendation in a 2025 guideline. Replaced with the FDA approval, which is the real and more useful news.

**corrected**

Real, presented at ESC 2025 with simultaneous publication in the NEW ENGLAND JOURNAL OF MEDICINE — the original cited The Lancet. Two other fixes: 'cutting major bleeding complications by more than half' is a specific magnitude that could not be confirmed, so it is now stated as reduced bleeding; and 'routinely stop aspirin 4 weeks after stenting' over-generalises a trial restricted to low-risk, completely revascularized, event-free patients. The design detail matters and has been added.

### 5. Early Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR) vs. Watchful Waiting in Asymptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis (EVOLVED Trial)

**corrected — original stated the opposite result**

The trial is real and correctly journaled and dated, but it was NEGATIVE. The original said early TAVR 'significantly lowers 3-year mortality and heart failure re-admission' and that guidelines shifted it from class IIb to class I. Neither is true: HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.44-1.43), P = .44, 'no demonstrable effect', and no guideline class change occurred. Two further points added: the intervention was aortic valve replacement generally, not TAVR specifically, and the trial recruited only 224 of 356 planned patients.

### 6. Discontinuation vs. Continuation of Aspirin in Patients on Chronic Oral Anticoagulation (OAC-ALONE 2 Trial)

**replaced — cited trial does not exist**

There is no 'OAC-ALONE 2' trial. The clinical teaching in the original was CORRECT and is supported — by AFIRE (NEJM 2019), which found monotherapy noninferior for efficacy, superior for safety, and with lower all-cause death. Only the citation is replaced. The real OAC-ALONE (2019) is mentioned because it is the trial the name was presumably reaching for, and its limitations explain why AFIRE is the one that settled the question.

### 9. Broad-Spectrum Anti-Inflammatory Therapy with Low-Dose Colchicine in Post-MI Patients (COLCOT-2 Trial)

**replaced — cited trial does not exist and the finding is contested**

There is no 'COLCOT-2' trial. The original said a large NEJM outcomes trial 'confirmed' that colchicine significantly reduces recurrent ischemic events and that guidelines elevated it to a routine recommendation. The large 2025 NEJM trial is CLEAR SYNERGY (OASIS 9) and it was NEUTRAL: HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.85-1.16) in 7,062 patients. COLCOT and LoDoCo2 were positive, so the entry now presents the genuine controversy rather than either side of it.

### 10. Intravenous Tenecteplase vs. Alteplase Prior to Endovascular Thrombectomy for Ischemic Stroke (TIMELESS-2 Trial)

**corrected — wrong trial name and wrong comparator**

There is no 'TIMELESS-2' trial. The real 2025 NEJM trial is BRIDGE-TNK (NEJMoa2503867), and the comparator matters: it compared tenecteplase PLUS thrombectomy against THROMBECTOMY ALONE, not tenecteplase against alteplase. The original's conclusion — that tenecteplase is preferred and the single bolus simplifies transfer — is defensible, but it does not follow from this trial in the way stated. Added the sICH and mortality numbers, which were both numerically higher with tenecteplase and were omitted entirely.
