## Citation not independently verified

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #4 — Stereotactic Radiosurgery versus Hippocampal-Avoidance Whole-Brain Radiotherapy for 5 to 20 Brain Metastases

**citation not independently verified**

The clinical question is real and actively studied — randomised comparisons of SRS against hippocampal-avoidance WBRT for patients with more than four brain metastases exist, and expanding the SRS indication beyond one to four lesions is a genuine direction of travel. But I did not verify this specific multicentre trial, its publication (the submission says 'ASCO/JAMA', which are different things), or its outcomes, so no result is asserted. The claim that it redefines ASTRO and NCCN brain metastasis protocols is likewise unconfirmed. Added the two practical costs the submission omits — higher surveillance imaging and salvage burden for new lesions outside treated sites, and planning workload scaling with lesion count — since those are what determine whether SRS is deliverable for a patient with 15 metastases.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #5 — Long-Term Outcomes of SBRT versus Surgery for Operable Early-Stage NSCLC

**citation not independently verified — one structural limitation added**

The comparison is real and long-standing, and SBRT's position as a radical option rather than only a salvage for medically inoperable patients is well argued in the literature. But I did not verify the specific ten-year randomised follow-up the submission describes, so no survival figures are quoted. ADDED, because the submission omits it and it is the standing objection: the randomised trials of SBRT versus surgery in OPERABLE patients closed early with small samples, so much comparative evidence is propensity-matched, and SBRT provides no pathological nodal staging — which affects adjuvant decision-making regardless of local control. Flagged for the physician to supply the primary citation.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #6 — Single-Fraction versus Conventional Postoperative Radiotherapy for Recurrent NSCLC

**citation not independently verified**

Extreme hypofractionation in lung SBRT is a real direction and single-institution randomised work in this area is plausible, but I did not verify this specific Roswell Park study, its sample size, its follow-up or its outcomes, so nothing is asserted beyond the comparison it describes. The submission's claim that it 'accelerates hypofractionation guidelines' is not supported by an institutional study. ADDED: the central-structure caution, because single-fraction delivery near proximal airway, oesophagus, brachial plexus or chest wall is where dose-per-fraction escalation causes harm, and late toxicity typically emerges after the local-control endpoint is reported — a point the submission's 'fewer acute toxicities' framing obscures.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #7 — Adjuvant Pelvic Radiotherapy After Radical Cystectomy in High-Risk Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer (BART)

**citation not independently verified — source is a conference-highlights blog**

The BART trial is a real trial name in this space and the question — adjuvant pelvic radiotherapy after cystectomy in high-risk muscle-invasive bladder cancer — is genuine. I did not verify the results, the primary endpoint, the sample size, or whether reduced locoregional recurrence came with any survival benefit, so the submission's 'proved that... significantly lowers local pelvic recurrence rates' is not asserted. The submitted link is a cancer-news roundup rather than a primary publication or ASTRO abstract. ADDED: the trade-off the submission omits, namely what an irradiated pelvis means for a urinary diversion and for future treatment options in that field, which is the reason adjuvant pelvic radiotherapy fell out of favour in the first place.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #8 — Genomic Classifier-Guided Use of ADT with Salvage Radiotherapy After Prostatectomy

**citation not independently verified — study design distinction added**

The field is real: genomic classifier and luminal/basal subtype analyses of randomised salvage radiotherapy cohorts have been published and are the basis for interest in biomarker-guided ADT. I did not verify the specific validation study, so no result is asserted, and the claim that clinical practice guidelines now recommend genomic profiling before prescribing hormone therapy with salvage radiation is unconfirmed. THE IMPORTANT ADDITION is a design distinction the submission elides: these are biomarker analyses OF randomised trials, not randomised trials OF biomarker-directed treatment. Nobody has randomised men to classifier-guided versus standard ADT decisions. The submission's 'allows doctors to avoid unnecessary hormone therapy' presents the stronger claim, and withholding ADT from a man with adverse clinical features on the strength of a subtype is not yet supported.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #10 — Olanzapine for Prevention of Radiotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting

**citation not independently verified**

The premise is well founded: olanzapine is established for chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting and is recommended in ASCO and MASCC/ESMO antiemetic guidance in that setting, so extending it to radiotherapy-induced nausea is a natural and plausible trial. I did not verify the specific phase III trial, its results, or that the guidelines have been updated to cover radiotherapy specifically — the submitted link is an ASCO press release listing noteworthy studies rather than the abstract or paper, so the effect size could not be checked. Rewritten to describe the intervention and the practical dosing point, which is the part a clinician needs and the submission omits: low doses of 2.5 to 5 mg are used to limit sedation, and additive sedation with opioids or benzodiazepines is the common problem.

## Wrong study or replaced

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #1 — Intensity-Modulated Proton Therapy versus IMRT for Oropharyngeal Cancer: Phase III Randomized Trial

**matched with corrections — verified, and I was wrong to doubt it**

VERIFIED, INCLUDING THE SURVIVAL CLAIM I EXPECTED TO FAIL. Having established for the otolaryngology list that TORPEdO's result was toxicity rather than survival, I expected a 10% absolute OS gain for protons to be an overstatement. It is not: this is a separate MD Anderson-led phase III trial, 440 patients (221 IMPT / 219 IMRT), randomised October 2013 to May 2022, published in The Lancet, with 5-year overall survival 90.9% versus 81.0% and a 33% relative / 13% absolute reduction in gastrostomy-tube dependence. It is described as the first trial to demonstrate a survival benefit for proton therapy. TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) The primary endpoint was PFS NON-INFERIORITY, which the trial met; overall survival is a secondary finding in an open-label design, and the entry says so because that is how the result will be challenged. (2) The submitted link is to proton-therapy.org, a proton therapy advocacy site, not The Lancet — replaced with The ASCO Post's report. Added the distinction from TORPEdO so the two trials are not conflated across the app.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #3 — Neoadjuvant 177Lu-PSMA Before Ablative Radiotherapy in Oligorecurrent Prostate Cancer (LUNAR)

**corrected — phase II not III, and one figure wrong**

Verified and the effect is real: the LUNAR trial, JCO 10.1200/JCO-25-01553, presented at ASTRO 2025, 92 patients, SBRT alone (47) versus two cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 then SBRT (45), median PFS 7.4 to 17.6 months, HR 0.37 (0.22-0.61), P<.0001, no significant added toxicity. FOUR CORRECTIONS. (1) PHASE: submitted as 'A Phase III trial'; LUNAR is phase II, which is the difference between justifying a confirmatory trial and changing practice. (2) FIGURE: 17.6 months, not the 17.3 submitted — and the submission writes '7.4 vs 17.3', which reads as though 7.4 were the treated arm. (3) SEQUENCE: the radioligand is neoadjuvant, given before SBRT, not combined with it. (4) The claim that it 'delays the need for androgen deprivation therapy by a median of 10 additional months' appears to restate the PFS difference as an ADT-deferral result; time to ADT was not confirmed as an endpoint, so it is flagged rather than asserted. Also removed 'establishes a new paradigm in... treatment guidelines' — no guideline follows from a 92-patient phase II.

## Corrected — dates, journals, numbers, strength of claim

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #2 — Omitting Regional Nodal Irradiation After Nodal Conversion to ypN0 (NSABP B-51 / RTOG 1304)

**matched with corrections — verified, guideline attribution softened**

Verified: NSABP B-51 / RTOG 1304, NEJMoa2414859, 1,556 patients with ypN0 after neoadjuvant chemotherapy randomised to regional nodal irradiation (772) or none (784), median follow-up 59.5 months, invasive breast cancer recurrence-free interval 92.7% versus 91.8% (HR 0.88, P = 0.51), 5-year overall survival 93.6% versus 94.0%. The submission's direction is exactly right and the figures have been added. TWO CORRECTIONS. (1) The submission titles this as 'ASTRO-ASCO-SSO Joint Postmastectomy Radiation Therapy Guidelines' and says it 'formally establishes' the omission; the trial is the evidence, and I could not confirm a 2025 joint guideline update incorporating it, so the trial is the anchor. (2) Added the eligibility boundary — clinically node-positive before chemotherapy, pathologically node-negative after — because that is the population, and the five-year follow-up limit, since a de-escalation decision turns on late as well as early recurrence.

### Radiation Oncology 2025 #9 — Stereotactic Arrhythmia Radioablation for Refractory Ventricular Arrhythmia

**corrected — evidence level and guideline status overstated**

STAR is real and the Washington University work the submission alludes to is genuine and important. But the submission calls it 'a landmark non-oncologic trial published in major medical literature' showing 'equivalent anti-arrhythmic control with far fewer treatment-related adverse events compared to catheter ablation', and says it is incorporated 'into multidisciplinary electrophysiology and radiation oncology consensus guidelines as a preferred non-invasive option'. I could not verify a randomised comparison against catheter ablation, and no consensus guideline designating STAR preferred was found — the published evidence is dominated by small single-centre series and early-phase studies in patients refractory to ablation. Rewritten to that evidence level. ADDED: the unresolved late-toxicity question, since the submission's '15-minute non-invasive intervention that avoids invasive catheter complications' frames a high single dose to the myocardium as though it carried no risk of its own.
