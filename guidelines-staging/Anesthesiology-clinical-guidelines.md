# Anesthesiology — Updated Clinical Guidelines

Breakthrough studies and consensus guidelines reshaping standards of practice, as they appear
in Rounds Codex Resident mode (Anesthesiology → Updated Clinical Guidelines).

Each entry carries a **citation check** recorded when the list was assembled. A check is a
literature search, not a medical re-read — see `VERIFICATION.md` for the method and its limits.

## 2025

### 1. Updated ASA Practice Consensus Guidelines on Preoperative Management of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

*Multisociety consensus (ASA, ESAIC and others) · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Shifted perioperative holding protocols for Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists from rigid, time-based holds (e.g. holding weekly doses for 7 days) to dynamic risk stratification based on GI symptom screening and point-of-care gastric ultrasound (POCUS).

**Clinical guideline impact.** Jointly adopted by the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) and the European Society of Anaesthesiology and Intensive Care (ESAIC), establishing standardized algorithms for delayed gastric emptying evaluation and aspiration risk mitigation.

**Practical implications.** Eliminates unnecessary surgical cancellations for asymptomatic patients on long-acting GLP-1 RAs while incorporating bedside gastric ultrasound into standard preoperative assessment when full holding times cannot be verified.

[ASA Guidelines on GLP-1 Receptor Agonists](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)

> **Citation check — matched.** The document exists: 'Multi-society clinical practice guidance for the safe use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists in the perioperative period.' Symptom screening + gastric POCUS + shared decision-making is an accurate description. DATE: released December 2024, not 2025.

### 2. Volatile Anesthetics vs. Total Intravenous Anesthesia (TIVA) for Recurrence-Free Survival After Cancer Surgery

*The Lancet · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** This large-scale multicenter trial in The Lancet provided definitive trial data comparing Sevoflurane-based volatile anesthesia against Propofol-based TIVA in patients undergoing major oncologic resections, demonstrating no statistically significant difference in 3-year recurrence-free or overall survival.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Directly influenced international perioperative oncology guidelines by removing recommendations that urged clinicians to favor TIVA solely for theoretical immunomodulatory or oncologic benefits.

**Practical implications.** Anesthesiologists can choose their primary maintenance technique based on patient hemodynamics, PONV risk profile, and drug availability rather than oncologic survival concerns.

[The Lancet - Volatile Anesthetics vs. TIVA in Cancer Surgery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=volatile+anaesthesia+versus+propofol+total+intravenous+anaesthesia+cancer+surgery+recurrence-free+survival+randomised)

> **Citation check — ⚠ NOT FOUND.** No such Lancet 2025 trial located. The two trials designed to answer this question -- GAS TIVA (22 sites, NSCLC) and VAPOR-C (lung/colorectal, 2x2 factorial) -- were still in progress or only just completing; neither has reported definitive 3-year recurrence-free survival in The Lancet. The claim of 'definitive trial data' and of guidelines being changed is unsupported.

### 3. Personalized Intraoperative Blood Pressure Management and Postoperative End-Organ Injury (HPI-SMART Trial)

*JAMA · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Published in JAMA, this study demonstrated that real-time, machine-learning-driven Hypotension Prediction Index (HPI) monitoring combined with individualized relative MAP targets significantly reduces the incidence and duration of postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI) and myocardial injury.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Updated international intraoperative hemodynamic standards to recommend targeting blood pressure relative to patient-specific baseline MAP (within 10-15%) rather than relying on arbitrary population thresholds (e.g. static MAP > 65 mmHg).

**Practical implications.** Encourages broader adoption of continuous arterial monitoring with predictive hemodynamic software during high-risk noncardiac procedures.

[JAMA - HPI-SMART Intraoperative Blood Pressure Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41076588/)

> **Citation check — ⚠ CONTRADICTED.** No trial named HPI-SMART exists. The real 2025 JAMA trial of individualized perioperative blood pressure management -- IMPROVE-multi -- reached the OPPOSITE conclusion: individualized MAP targets based on preoperative mean nighttime MAP did NOT reduce the composite of AKI, myocardial injury, cardiac arrest or death at 7 days versus routine management at MAP >= 65 mmHg. The url field currently points at IMPROVE-multi, i.e. at the real study with the opposite result -- the summary text above does not describe it.

### 4. 2025 ASA Practice Guidelines for Neuromuscular Blockade Monitoring and Reversal Standards

*Anesthesiology (ASA practice guideline) · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Mandated objective, quantitative neuromuscular transmission monitoring (TOF ≥ 0.90) at the adductor pollicis prior to extubation, declaring subjective visual or tactile Train-of-Four (TOF) evaluation insufficient to rule out residual paralysis.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Formalized Sugammadex as the first-line reversal agent for aminosteroidal neuromuscular blockers while redefining institutional safety metrics around residual neuromuscular blockade.

**Practical implications.** Surgical facilities are updating OR suites with quantitative acceleromyography or electromyography monitors and mandating numerical TOF ratio documentation before tracheal extubation.

[Anesthesiology - Quantitative Neuromuscular Monitoring Guidelines](https://www.asahq.org/standards-and-practice-parameters/practice-guideline-monitoring-and-antagonism-of-neuromuscular-blockade)

> **Citation check — matched.** The ASA 'Practice Guideline for Monitoring and Antagonism of Neuromuscular Blockade' is real and every clinical claim here checks out: quantitative monitoring at the adductor pollicis to a TOF ratio >= 0.9 before extubation, sugammadex over neostigmine at deep/moderate/shallow block, neostigmine reasonable at minimal depth. DATE: this is the guideline published in 2023, not a new 2025 document.

### 5. Restrictive vs. Liberal Oxygenation Targets During Mechanical Ventilation in Major Surgery

*NEJM · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Published in NEJM, this pragmatic trial proved that intraoperative hyperoxia (PaO₂ > 150 mmHg) increases systemic oxidative stress and postoperative pulmonary complications without offering any protective reduction in surgical site infections.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Rewrote intraoperative oxygen titration guidelines across major societies, establishing a target SpO₂ range of 92%-96% and discouraging unguided, high FiO₂ administration.

**Practical implications.** Requires clinicians to actively down-titrate FiO₂ post-intubation rather than leaving oxygen blenders at standard high default settings.

[NEJM - Intraoperative Oxygenation Targets Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=intraoperative+restrictive+versus+liberal+oxygenation+targets+randomized+trial+postoperative+pulmonary+complications)

> **Citation check — ⚠ NOT FOUND / PARTLY CONTRADICTED.** No such NEJM 2025 pragmatic trial located. The 'no SSI benefit from high FiO2' half is supported (meta-analysis of 30 studies, 18,055 patients: RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.79-1.03). But a 2025 meta-analysis of 20 RCTs (5,793 patients) found low FiO2 gave LESS atelectasis yet MORE acute kidney injury (RR 1.64, 95% CI 1.15-2.34), and a noninferiority RCT found high FiO2 noninferior for pulmonary complications. No society has adopted a 92-96% intraoperative SpO2 target on the strength of a single trial.

### 6. Joint Consensus Statement on Perioperative SGLT2 Inhibitor Management and Euglycemic DKA Prevention

*Anaesthesia (multidisciplinary consensus) · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Provided clear, evidence-based holding schedules (3-4 days prior to elective surgery) for sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors and established a standardized pathway for evaluating intraoperative blood beta-hydroxybutyrate.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Endorsed by Circulation and major anesthesia societies, establishing unified protocols across cardiology, endocrinology, and anesthesiology to prevent euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (eDKA).

**Practical implications.** Provides a structured algorithm for handling urgent/emergent procedures where SGLT2 inhibitors could not be held, including routine metabolic panel and blood ketone screening.

[Circulation - SGLT2 Inhibitor Perioperative Management](https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/anae.16541)

> **Citation check — ⚠ matched with corrections.** A real 2025 multidisciplinary consensus exists -- El-Boghdadly et al., 'Elective peri-operative management of adults taking GLP-1 receptor agonists, GIP agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors', Anaesthesia 2025 -- and it recommends a 3-day hold. TWO CORRECTIONS NEEDED: it was published in Anaesthesia, NOT endorsed by Circulation; and the hold is 3 days (the FDA separately says at least 4-6), not '3-4 days'. Note also that the consensus itself states there is no Class 1 evidence in this area.

### 7. Restrictive vs. Liberal Transfusion Strategy in Patients Undergoing Major Cardiac Surgery (TITRE-3 Trial)

*NEJM · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Demonstrating in NEJM that a restrictive red blood cell transfusion threshold (7.5 g/dL) is non-inferior to a liberal threshold (9.5 g/dL) regarding 90-day major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), even in high-risk cardiac surgery patients.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Updated Perioperative Patient Blood Management (PBM) guidelines, lowering recommended hemoglobin transfusion triggers across cardiac and complex vascular procedures.

**Practical implications.** Significantly reduces blood product utilization, minimizes transfusion-related adverse events (TRALI/TACO), and optimizes blood bank inventory management.

[NEJM - TITRE-3 Cardiac Surgery Transfusion Trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1711818)

> **Citation check — ⚠ WRONG TRIAL / WRONG YEAR.** No trial named TITRE-3 exists. The description is TRICS III, NEJM 2017: >5,000 moderate-to-high-risk on-pump cardiac surgery patients, restrictive < 7.5 g/dL vs liberal < 9.5 g/dL in the OR, noninferior for the composite (11.4% vs 12.5%, within the 3% margin), transfusion 52.3% vs 72.6%. The clinical content is correct; the name, the year and the '90-day MACCE' framing are not. The url now points at TRICS III. (TITRe2 is a separate, earlier UK trial.)

### 8. Regional Anesthesia with Sedation vs. General Anesthesia for Delirium Prevention in Elderly Hip Fracture Surgery (RACE-2 Trial)

*JAMA Surgery · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Published in JAMA Surgery, this multicenter randomized trial showed no difference in postoperative delirium rates between spinal anesthesia and light-depth general anesthesia when general anesthesia is guided by processed EEG (bispectral index).

**Clinical guideline impact.** Reframed geriatric perioperative neurocognitive guidelines, shifting the emphasis from the choice of regional versus general technique toward active depth-of-anesthesia monitoring to prevent burst suppression.

**Practical implications.** Gives anesthesiologists flexibility to choose spinal or general techniques based on patient anatomy and hemodynamic tolerance without compromising neurocognitive safety.

[JAMA Surgery - RACE-2 Delirium Trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2787494)

> **Citation check — ⚠ NOT FOUND.** No trial named RACE-2 located. The 'no difference in delirium between spinal and general' conclusion IS well supported, but by REGAIN (NEJM 2021; delirium 33.9% vs 37.8% in the cognitively impaired, 16.2% vs 16.0% in the unimpaired) and RAGA (JAMA 2022), plus several meta-analyses -- not by a 2025 JAMA Surgery trial, and not with processed-EEG-guided GA as the comparator. The url now points at RAGA.

### 9. Focused Update on Perioperative High-Sensitivity Cardiac Troponin (hs-cTn) Surveillance in Noncardiac Surgery

*Circulation / JACC (AHA/ACC guideline) · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Defined management protocols for Perioperative Myocardial Injury/Infarction in Noncardiac Surgery (MINS) following routine baseline and postoperative day 1-2 high-sensitivity troponin monitoring.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Joint AHA/ACC and ESAIC update recommending baseline and routine post-op hs-cTn screening for patients aged ≥ 65 or those with documented cardiovascular risk undergoing moderate-to-high-risk surgery.

**Practical implications.** Enables early detection of silent MINS, triggering early post-op cardiology evaluation, optimized antiplatelet strategies, and statin initiation that measurably lowers 1-year mortality.

[Circulation - Focused Update on Perioperative Troponin Surveillance](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.06.013)

> **Citation check — ⚠ matched with corrections.** The substance is right and lives in the 2024 AHA/ACC/ACS/ASNC/HRS/SCA/SCCT/SCMR/SVM Guideline for Perioperative Cardiovascular Management for Noncardiac Surgery: preoperative cTn is reasonable in known CVD, or age >= 65, or age >= 45 with symptoms, undergoing elevated-risk surgery. CORRECTIONS: it is a 2024 full guideline, not a 2025 focused update, and not joint with ESAIC. The '1-year mortality' claim is the strongest statement here and is not something the guideline asserts.

### 10. Point-of-Care Viscoelastic Testing-Guided Fibrinogen Administration in Severe Postpartum Hemorrhage

*The Lancet · 2025*

**The breakthrough.** A major multicenter trial in The Lancet proved that rapid, point-of-care Viscoelastic Hemostatic Assay (VHA)-guided targeted fibrinogen replacement reduces ICU admissions and overall blood product exposure compared to empirical fixed-ratio blood component administration during severe obstetric hemorrhage.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Updated Obstetric Hemorrhage Taskforce standards to strongly recommend VHA (ROTEM/TEG)-driven fibrinogen concentrate administration over empirical fresh frozen plasma resuscitation.

**Practical implications.** Accelerates precise coagulopathy correction on labor and delivery units, reducing volume overload risks and delays caused by plasma thawing.

[The Lancet - Fibrinogen Replacement in Obstetric Hemorrhage](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000709121753754X)

> **Citation check — ⚠ CONTRADICTED.** No such Lancet 2025 trial located. The one randomised trial of viscoelastometric-guided early fibrinogen concentrate in PPH -- OBS2, BJA 2017, double-blind -- found NO benefit: adjusted IRR for allogeneic units 0.72 (95% CI 0.3-1.7), P = 0.45, and concluded that fibrinogen concentrate triggered by FIBTEM A5 <= 15 mm did not improve outcomes. FIDEL likewise did not show benefit from early systematic fibrinogen. The url now points at OBS2, the real study with the opposite result.

## 2026

10 entries · **7 with a citation problem — see the checks below**

### 1. 2026 American Society of Anesthesiologists Practice Guideline on Perioperative Pain Management Using Local and Regional Analgesia for Cardiothoracic Surgeries, Mastectomy, and Abdominal Surgeries

*Anesthesiology · January 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Established evidence-based, procedure-specific recommendations for fascial plane blocks (e.g. TAP, ESP, serratus anterior, and parasternal plane blocks) across cardiothoracic, mastectomy, and abdominal surgical procedures.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Issued by the ASA Task Force on Perioperative Pain Management, elevating ultrasound-guided fascial plane blocks to a "Strong Recommendation" status for open surgeries in both adult and pediatric populations.

**Practical implications.** Demonstrates a significant reduction in 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption (reducing up to 60 mg oral morphine equivalents) and rest/dynamic pain scores, solidifying fascial plane blocks as a cornerstone of standardized opioid-sparing multimodal pathways.

[ASA Guidelines on Regional Analgesia and Perioperative Pain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41363869/)

> **Citation check — ⚠ matched with corrections.** Real: Anesthesiology, January 2026, PMID 41363869. CORRECTION TO THE STRENGTH CLAIM: for ADULTS the Task Force recommendation is CONDITIONAL, and only for minimally invasive cardiothoracic surgery and open hernia repair. The STRONG recommendation applies to CHILDREN after open cardiac or thoracic surgery. The guideline also states its own analysis was limited by low methodologic quality, inconsistent outcome measurement and small single-centre samples -- worth keeping, since residents will be taught from this.

### 2. Intervals for Oscillometric Arterial Pressure Monitoring during Noncardiac Surgery: The "2.5-Minute versus 5-Minute" Randomized Clinical Trial

*Anesthesiology · 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Evaluated whether increasing non-invasive oscillometric blood pressure cycle frequency from the traditional 5-minute interval down to 2.5 minutes significantly reduces intraoperative hypotension exposure under general anesthesia.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Challenges the historic 5-minute standard blood pressure monitoring interval in noncardiac surgery guidelines by proving that shorter intervals detect transient drops much earlier, reducing total hypotensive depth and duration.

**Practical implications.** Prompts clinicians to shorten automated NIBP cycling to 2.5 minutes during high-risk surgical phases and induction, bridging the safety gap when invasive continuous arterial monitoring is not indicated.

[Anesthesiology - 2.5-Minute vs 5-Minute Blood Pressure Monitoring Trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06314074)

> **Citation check — ⚠ RESULT INVERTED.** The trial is real (NCT06314074, 264 patients, single centre, published in Anesthesiology with an accompanying editorial) but it was NEGATIVE. Cycling at 2.5 vs 5.0 min did NOT reduce the time-weighted average of MAP < 65 mmHg -- both arms were nearly identical and very low, most likely because 98% of patients were on norepinephrine infusions. Severe hypotension (MAP < 50) was 5.3% vs 9.9%, P = 0.24, not significant. The 'proving that shorter intervals... reduce total hypotensive depth and duration' claim and the recommendation to shorten cycling both state the opposite of the finding.

### 3. Videolaryngoscopy versus Direct Laryngoscopy for Double-Lumen Tube Intubation: The DOLVI Multicentre Randomized Trial

*British Journal of Anaesthesia · 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Published in the British Journal of Anaesthesia, this multicenter trial proved that videolaryngoscopy significantly improves first-attempt intubation success (84.7% vs 76.9%) and glottic exposure during double-lumen tube (DLT) placement for thoracic surgical lung isolation.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Directly updates thoracic airway management guidelines, establishing videolaryngoscopy as the preferred primary modality for anatomical lung isolation.

**Practical implications.** Reduces procedural difficulty, loss of glottic view, esophageal intubation rates, and airway trauma, establishing a faster, safer pathway to secure single-lung ventilation.

[British Journal of Anaesthesia - DOLVI Multicentre Trial](https://www.bjanaesthesia.org/article/S0007-0912(26)00207-2/abstract)

> **Citation check — matched.** Real: DOuble-Lumen intubation with VIdeolaryngoscopy (DOLVI), BJA 2026, four Spanish hospitals, Fernandez et al., with an accompanying editorial ('Videolaryngoscopy: the new standard for double-lumen tube placement?'). First-attempt success was the primary outcome; glottic visualisation, oesophageal intubation and tube malposition were secondary and all favoured videolaryngoscopy. Minor note: 'directly updates thoracic airway management guidelines' is the trialists' and editorialist's direction of travel rather than a guideline change that has already happened.

### 4. Management of Direct Oral Anticoagulants in Adult Patients Undergoing Cardiac Surgery: A Joint Consensus Statement by the SCA and STS

*Anesthesia & Analgesia / The Annals of Thoracic Surgery · April 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Established unified timing, laboratory monitoring, and reversal algorithms for direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) in adult cardiac surgical patients undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Jointly published by the Society of Cardiovascular Anesthesiologists (SCA) and Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS), standardizing preoperative hold duration based on renal function and providing clear criteria for targeted reversal agents versus prothrombin complex concentrates (PCC).

**Practical implications.** Reduces chest tube bleeding, transfusion requirements, and emergency re-operation rates while eliminating unnecessary delay for urgent cardiac procedures.

[Anesthesia & Analgesia - SCA/STS Joint DOAC Guidelines](https://journals.lww.com/anesthesia-analgesia/fulltext/2026/04000/management_of_direct_oral_anticoagulants_in_adult.10.aspx)

> **Citation check — matched.** Real: published 29 January 2026, appearing in the April 2026 issues of both Anesthesia & Analgesia and The Annals of Thoracic Surgery (121:774-789). Covers cessation before surgery, monitoring options, bridging, reversal agents and resumption -- exactly the domains described. The 'reduces chest tube bleeding, transfusion and re-operation' line is the consensus's stated goal, not a measured outcome.

### 5. Efficacy and Safety of Remimazolam versus Propofol for Prolonged Postoperative Mechanical Ventilation: A Phase 3 Trial

*Anesthesiology · 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Published in Anesthesiology, this multicenter trial demonstrated that the soft-drug, organ-independent benzodiazepine remimazolam provides non-inferior sedation efficacy to propofol with significantly lower rates of hypotension in patients requiring postop ventilatory support.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Formally integrates remimazolam into post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) and surgical ICU guidelines as a flumazenil-reversible alternative to propofol for postop mechanical ventilation.

**Practical implications.** Reduces intra-procedural vasopressor dependence and eliminates risks associated with propofol infusion syndrome (PRIS) and hypertriglyceridemia during extended postoperative sedation.

[Anesthesiology - Remimazolam vs Propofol Phase 3 Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41855428/)

> **Citation check — ⚠ matched with corrections.** Real: 'Efficacy and Safety of Remimazolam Tosylate versus Propofol for Sedation of Postoperative Mechanically Ventilated Patients in Intensive Care Units: A Multicenter, Randomized, Single-blind, Noninferiority, Phase 3 Trial', PMID 41855428 (NCT06222294). CORRECTIONS: the trial studied SHORT-TERM sedation, not 'prolonged' -- the title as written overstates the duration, and 'extended postoperative sedation' in the practical implications does too. Lower hypotension with remimazolam is supported by companion trials. 'Formally integrates into PACU and surgical ICU guidelines' has not happened -- no society guideline has been revised on this.

### 6. Guidelines to the Practice of Anesthesia (Revised 2026 Edition)

*Canadian Journal of Anesthesia · January 2026*

**The breakthrough.** A comprehensive overhaul of basic anesthesia care standards by the Canadian Anesthesiologists' Society (CAS), featuring new requirements for continuous capnography in all sedation locations and environmental sustainability standards.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Updates universal practice standards to require continuous micro-stream capnography during deep sedation outside the operating room (NORA) and formalizes institutional plans to phase out high-global-warming volatile agents (e.g. desflurane).

**Practical implications.** Accelerates the elimination of high-carbon anesthetic gases globally while enforcing stricter capnographic safety monitoring across gastrointestinal and interventional suites.

[Guidelines to the Practice of Anesthesia 2026 Edition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12630-026-03075-w)

> **Citation check — ⚠ document matched, specifics UNVERIFIED.** The document is real: CAS Guidelines to the Practice of Anesthesia, Revised Edition 2026, Can J Anesth, January 2026, PMID 41820767 -- the 49th annual revision and the 18th consecutive year in CJA. BUT 'a comprehensive overhaul' is the wrong characterisation: CAS states that existing sections are revised annually only where high-quality literature supports it, with new sections added as needed. The two specific claims -- a new continuous-capnography requirement for all sedation locations, and formal desflurane phase-out plans -- could not be confirmed from the abstract and need checking against the document itself.

### 7. Electroencephalographic Monitoring in the Recovery Room for Identification of Patients at Risk for Postoperative Delirium

*Anesthesiology · June 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Demonstrated that automated frontal EEG monitoring during the immediate post-anesthesia recovery phase detects persistent subclinical slow-wave activity, accurately identifying patients at high risk for delirium before clinical symptoms emerge.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Refines perioperative brain health guidelines by recommending post-anesthesia EEG screening in the recovery room to trigger targeted non-pharmacological delirium bundles.

**Practical implications.** Enables PACU nursing and anesthesia teams to initiate early delirium precautions - such as reorientation, structured pain protocols, and avoiding anticholinergics - before patient transfer to surgical wards.

[Anesthesiology - PACU EEG Delirium Risk Screening](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41983562/)

> **Citation check — ⚠ matched with corrections.** Real: PMID 41983562, June 2026, Charite-Universitatsmedizin Berlin with Harvard/MIT. The finding is that recovery-room frontal EEG identifies a 'vulnerable brain' phenotype associated with increased delirium risk, and that integrating it into postoperative care MAY support risk stratification. CORRECTIONS: 'accurately identifying' overstates a risk-association study, and no guideline has been refined -- the authors propose this, they do not report a guideline change.

### 8. Long-Term Antiplatelet Monotherapy After PCI: 10-Year Outcomes of the HOST-EXAM Trial

*The Lancet · March 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Published in The Lancet, this 10-year randomized trial showed that long-term clopidogrel monotherapy maintains superior reductions in major adverse cardiovascular events and bleeding compared to aspirin monotherapy in post-PCI patients.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Updates perioperative antiplatelet consensus recommendations regarding the long-term management of patients with drug-eluting stents presenting for elective noncardiac surgery.

**Practical implications.** Clarifies preoperative hold and restart timelines for chronic P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy, reducing ischemic cardiac events and surgical bleeding complications.

[The Lancet - 10-Year Follow-up of HOST-EXAM Trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)00422-8/abstract)

> **Citation check — matched.** Real: 'Aspirin versus clopidogrel for chronic maintenance monotherapy after percutaneous coronary intervention: 10-year follow-up of the HOST-EXAM trial', Lancet 2026, presented at ACC 2026. 5,438 South Korean patients post-DES PCI who had completed 6-18 months of DAPT; median follow-up 10.5 years, 92.8% complete. Clopidogrel lower for the primary composite (25.4% vs 28.5%, HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77-0.96, p = 0.0050) and for ischaemic and bleeding outcomes, but NOT for all-cause mortality -- worth stating, since 'superior reductions' alone implies a mortality benefit that was not found. The perioperative-guideline and hold-timeline implications are extrapolation, not trial findings.

### 9. Expert Consensus Statement on the Perioperative Management of Adult Patients Undergoing High-Risk Thyroidectomy

*Anesthesia & Analgesia · June 2026*

**The breakthrough.** A multi-society expert consensus statement establishing standardized neural monitoring (IONM) protocols, vocal cord assessment algorithms, and acute post-thyroidectomy hematoma emergency pathways.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Issued in Anesthesia & Analgesia, establishing standardized endotracheal tube positioning protocols for intraoperative recurrent laryngeal nerve monitoring and bed-side wound decompression algorithms.

**Practical implications.** Significantly reduces permanent vocal cord paralysis rates and improves rapid response times for post-thyroidectomy airway compromise on surgical floors.

[Anesthesia & Analgesia - High-Risk Thyroidectomy Perioperative Consensus](https://doi.org/10.1213/ANE.0000000000008141)

> **Citation check — ⚠ matched with corrections.** Real: Anesthesia & Analgesia, published 10 June 2026, DOI 10.1213/ANE.0000000000008141. CORRECTION: it is from the Society for Head and Neck Anesthesia specifically, not 'multi-society'. The 'significantly reduces permanent vocal cord paralysis rates' claim is an expected benefit of the consensus, not a measured outcome -- a consensus statement cannot demonstrate a rate reduction.

### 10. EEG-Guided Emergence Protocols and the Quality of Post-Anesthetic Recovery and Dreaming

*Anesthesiology · July 2026*

**The breakthrough.** Demonstrated that a standardized, EEG-guided emergence bundle facilitates smooth emergence transitions out of general anesthesia, markedly reducing emergence agitation and post-traumatic stress responses.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Introduces processed EEG spectral emergence tracking into post-anesthesia recovery standards to prevent burst suppression during emergence.

**Practical implications.** Gives clinicians objective biophysical feedback to time anesthetic wash-out, resulting in calmer emergence transitions, higher patient satisfaction, and lower PACU nursing burden.

[Anesthesiology - EEG-Guided Emergence and Recovery Quality](https://journals.lww.com/anesthesiology/fulltext/10.1097/aln.0000000000005968~feasibility-of-a-multicomponent-protocol-to-promote-dreaming)

> **Citation check — ⚠ OVERSTATED.** The study is real -- 'Feasibility of a Multicomponent Protocol to Promote Dreaming during Surgical Anesthesia', Anesthesiology, July 2026 -- but it is a FEASIBILITY study whose endpoint was DREAMING, not delirium, agitation or PTSD. Five elements: preinduction verbal priming about dreaming, propofol as the emergence anesthetic, EEG-guided emergence, >= 10 min of minimised stimulation before emergence, and immediate postemergence interviews. Results: 69% of 452 interviewed patients dreamed, 93% among the 57 with full protocol adherence; 86% of dreams positive, none very negative; dreamers reported better sleep quality (9.16 +/- 1.57 vs 7.65 +/- 2.86); safe, no awareness, no difference in recovery times or analgesic/antiemetic use. It did NOT measure emergence agitation or PTSD, and nothing has entered 'post-anesthesia recovery standards'.
