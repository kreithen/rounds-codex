### 1. 2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI Guideline for the Management of Patients With Acute Coronary Syndromes

*Circulation / JACC · February 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Consolidated STEMI and non-ST-elevation ACS into a single unified pathway, replacing the 2013 STEMI guideline, the 2014 NSTE-ACS guideline and the 2015 primary PCI focused update. Recommends the potent P2Y12 inhibitors ticagrelor or prasugrel over clopidogrel, and early high-intensity statin with ezetimibe co-initiation rather than sequential add-on.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Issued jointly by the ACC, AHA, ACEP, NAEMSP and SCAI, so it speaks to prehospital care, the emergency department and the cath lab in one document. Updates medical therapy, invasive catheterization timelines and bleeding-risk reduction together rather than in separate silos.

**Practical implications.** Recommends upfront dual lipid lowering rather than waiting to see whether a statin alone gets the LDL to goal, and a PPI alongside dual antiplatelet therapy in patients at high GI bleeding risk. Treating ACS as one syndrome with one pathway is the practical shift here.

[Circulation - 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)

> **Citation check — matched.** Real and accurately summarised. Circulation DOI 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309, also JACC 2025;85(22):2135-2237, PMID 40014670. The five-society authorship, the consolidation of the three prior documents, potent P2Y12 preference and ezetimibe co-initiation all check out. Only change: 'Mandates' softened to 'Recommends' — a guideline recommends; it does not mandate.

### 2. Efficacy and Safety of Baxdrostat in Uncontrolled and Resistant Hypertension (BaxHTN)

*NEJM · 30 August 2025*

**The breakthrough.** A phase 3 trial of baxdrostat, a selective aldosterone synthase inhibitor, added to background therapy in uncontrolled or resistant hypertension. Seated systolic BP fell 14.5 mmHg (1 mg) and 15.7 mmHg (2 mg) versus 5.8 mmHg with placebo at 12 weeks, and roughly 40% reached systolic below 130 mmHg versus 18.7% on placebo.

**Clinical guideline impact.** The first new antihypertensive mechanism in years to deliver effect sizes of this magnitude in resistant disease. The FDA approved baxdrostat on 18 May 2026 as the first aldosterone synthase inhibitor, for adults with uncontrolled hypertension on two or more agents. It was not yet in the 2025 AHA/ACC hypertension guideline, which published around the same time as the trial.

**Practical implications.** A targeted option for patients who fail triple therapy including a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist. Hyperkalemia DID occur — 2.3% at 1 mg and 3.0% at 2 mg — so potassium still needs monitoring; the trial's finding was that it was manageable, not absent.

[NEJM - BaxHTN baxdrostat trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2507109)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected.** Real (NEJMoa2507109) and the efficacy claim holds, with numbers now added. Two fixes. The original said baxdrostat caused no clinically significant hyperkalemia — elevated potassium occurred in 2.3-3.0%. And it said the trial 'directly influenced updated AHA/ACC hypertension guidelines, adding aldosterone synthase inhibition as a novel class recommendation'; the drug was not FDA-approved until May 2026, so it could not have been a class recommendation in a 2025 guideline. Replaced with the FDA approval, which is the real and more useful news.

### 3. Early Aspirin Discontinuation after PCI in Low-Risk Acute Myocardial Infarction (TARGET-FIRST)

*NEJM · ESC Congress, September 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Forty European sites. Patients with STEMI or NSTEMI who had complete revascularization within 7 days using a contemporary drug-eluting stent AND completed one month of DAPT event-free were randomized to continue DAPT or switch to P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy for 11 months. The composite of death, MI, stent thrombosis, stroke or major bleeding at 11 months was 2.1% with monotherapy versus 2.2% with DAPT — noninferior, with less bleeding.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Adds to the body of evidence supporting aspirin withdrawal at one month in selected patients, and is part of why post-PCI guidance continues to move toward shorter DAPT with P2Y12 monotherapy.

**Practical implications.** Note who was enrolled: LOW-RISK patients, completely revascularized, who had already gone a month without an event. That is the population the result applies to — it is not a licence to stop aspirin at four weeks in everyone. The very low event rate in both arms (about 2%) is itself the signal that this was a low-risk cohort.

[ESC - TARGET-FIRST trial](https://www.escardio.org/news/press/press-releases/Early-aspirin-discontinuation-linked-to-benefits-in-low-risk-patients-with-myocardial-infarction-undergoing-coronary-stenting/)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected.** Real, presented at ESC 2025 with simultaneous publication in the NEW ENGLAND JOURNAL OF MEDICINE — the original cited The Lancet. Two other fixes: 'cutting major bleeding complications by more than half' is a specific magnitude that could not be confirmed, so it is now stated as reduced bleeding; and 'routinely stop aspirin 4 weeks after stenting' over-generalises a trial restricted to low-risk, completely revascularized, event-free patients. The design detail matters and has been added.

### 4. Tirzepatide in Patients with Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction and Obesity (SUMMIT)

*NEJM · November 2024 (AHA Scientific Sessions)*

**The breakthrough.** The dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide in 731 patients with HFpEF and obesity reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure by 38%, with a 46% relative reduction in worsening HF events, alongside 12-21% weight loss and improved exercise tolerance. A CMR substudy showed LV mass down 11 g and paracardiac adipose tissue down 45 mL.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Strong support for treating obesity as a driver of the HFpEF phenotype rather than a comorbidity beside it. Tirzepatide is NOT yet approved for obesity-related HFpEF — the manufacturer has stated it intends to seek approval — and no heart failure guideline currently recommends it as disease-modifying therapy for this indication.

**Practical implications.** Makes a strong case for metabolic treatment in obese HFpEF, and the structural findings (less LV mass, less paracardiac fat) suggest the mechanism is more than symptom relief from weight loss. At 731 patients the trial is moderate in size, so guideline bodies are waiting on more before changing recommendations.

[NEJM - SUMMIT trial, tirzepatide in HFpEF](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.11.001)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected.** Real and the 38% figure is right. Two fixes. DATE: presented at AHA and published in NEJM in November 2024, not 2025. And the original said it 'Rewrote Heart Failure Society guidelines to recommend dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists alongside SGLT2 inhibitors as primary disease-modifying therapy' — that has not happened, and the drug is not approved for this indication. Trial size and the CMR substudy findings added. The url points at the JACC CMR substudy, which is openly accessible; the parent trial is NEJM 2024.

### 5. Early Intervention in Patients With Asymptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis and Myocardial Fibrosis (EVOLVED)

*JAMA · January 2025*

**The breakthrough.** A negative trial. In asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis with myocardial fibrosis on cardiac MRI, early aortic valve intervention had NO demonstrable effect on all-cause death or unplanned AS-related hospitalization: 18% (20/113) versus 23% (25/111), hazard ratio 0.79 (95% CI 0.44-1.43), P = .44. Twenty-four centres in the UK and Australia.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Does not support moving early intervention up the recommendation ladder. The trial enrolled 224 of a planned 356 patients, so it is underpowered and the confidence interval is wide — the authors themselves call for further research rather than claiming equivalence. Fibrosis-triggered early intervention remains an open question, not a settled one.

**Practical implications.** Continue guideline-directed surveillance in asymptomatic severe AS. MRI-detected fibrosis is a genuine marker of risk, but this trial does not show that acting on it early improves outcomes — and a wide confidence interval around a null result is not the same as proof of no benefit.

[JAMA - EVOLVED randomized clinical trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2825540)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected — original stated the opposite result.** The trial is real and correctly journaled and dated, but it was NEGATIVE. The original said early TAVR 'significantly lowers 3-year mortality and heart failure re-admission' and that guidelines shifted it from class IIb to class I. Neither is true: HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.44-1.43), P = .44, 'no demonstrable effect', and no guideline class change occurred. Two further points added: the intervention was aortic valve replacement generally, not TAVR specifically, and the trial recruited only 224 of 356 planned patients.

### 6. Antithrombotic Therapy for Atrial Fibrillation with Stable Coronary Disease (AFIRE)

*NEJM · 2019*

**The breakthrough.** Rivaroxaban monotherapy was noninferior to rivaroxaban plus an antiplatelet agent for efficacy in atrial fibrillation with stable coronary disease, and SUPERIOR for safety — major bleeding was lower on monotherapy, and so was death from any cause. The trial was stopped early because of the mortality difference.

**Clinical guideline impact.** The evidence behind de-escalating to oral anticoagulant monotherapy beyond a year after PCI or ACS in patients with AF. AFIRE is the definitive trial here: the earlier OAC-ALONE trial addressed the same question but stopped at 696 of 2,000 planned patients and was inconclusive, and only a quarter of its patients were on a DOAC.

**Practical implications.** Deprescribe routine daily aspirin in patients established on full-dose oral anticoagulation for AF once they are beyond the early post-stent period. Adding an antiplatelet to an anticoagulant in stable coronary disease buys bleeding without buying ischemic protection.

[NEJM - AFIRE trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1904143)

> **Citation check — ⚠ replaced — cited trial does not exist.** There is no 'OAC-ALONE 2' trial. The clinical teaching in the original was CORRECT and is supported — by AFIRE (NEJM 2019), which found monotherapy noninferior for efficacy, superior for safety, and with lower all-cause death. Only the citation is replaced. The real OAC-ALONE (2019) is mentioned because it is the trial the name was presumably reaching for, and its limitations explain why AFIRE is the one that settled the question.

### 7. Optical Coherence Tomography-Guided versus Angiography-Guided PCI for Complex Lesions (OCCUPI)

*The Lancet · 2024 (ESC Congress)*

**The breakthrough.** An investigator-initiated, multicentre, randomised, open-label superiority trial at 20 South Korean hospitals. 1,604 patients requiring drug-eluting stents for complex lesions were randomised to OCT-guided (n=803) or angiography-guided PCI (n=801). Major adverse cardiac events at one year: 5% (37 patients) versus 7% (59 patients) — a 38% relative reduction.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Together with OCTOBER (complex bifurcations) and ILUMIEN IV, this is the evidence base behind the strong recommendation for intravascular imaging in complex PCI. Five-year outcomes and network meta-analyses of IVUS versus OCT versus angiography have since been published.

**Practical implications.** Argues for routine intravascular imaging rather than relying on the angiographic luminogram alone in bifurcations, left main and chronic total occlusions — the angiogram cannot show stent underexpansion or malapposition, which is what drives the difference.

[The Lancet - OCCUPI trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(24)01454-5/abstract)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected — trial name and year.** There is no 'OCCAMI' trial. The description matches OCCUPI, The Lancet 2024. The teaching is correct and unchanged; name, year and numbers are now right. One softening: the original claimed significantly lower rates of target vessel failure, stent thrombosis AND cardiac death individually — the primary endpoint was the composite MACE, and individual components in a trial this size are not separately powered.

### 8. Left Atrial Appendage Closure versus Best Medical Therapy in AF at High Risk of Stroke and Bleeding (CLOSURE-AF)

*Presented AHA Scientific Sessions · 8 November 2025*

**The breakthrough.** A negative trial, and a surprising one. 912 patients across 42 sites with AF at high stroke risk (CHA₂DS₂-VASc > 2) AND high bleeding risk (HAS-BLED > 3, prior major or intracranial bleeding, or CrCl 15-29 mL/min) were randomised to catheter-based left atrial appendage closure or best medical care including a DOAC where eligible. LAAC was NOT noninferior to medical therapy — physician-directed medical care performed better.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Argues against expanding LAAC as a first-line alternative to anticoagulation in high-bleeding-risk AF. This is the population in which LAAC was most expected to win, which is what makes the result important.

**Practical implications.** Do not treat a high HAS-BLED score as an indication for LAAC in preference to anticoagulation. LAAC retains a role in patients who genuinely cannot take an anticoagulant, but this trial does not support offering it as an early alternative to patients who can.

[ACC - CLOSURE-AF trial results](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/clinical-trials/2026/03/03/18/35/closure-af)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected — original stated the opposite result.** The trial is real (AHA, 8 November 2025, Landmesser, 912 patients, 42 sites) but the result is the REVERSE of the original entry. The original said it proved noninferiority for stroke prevention with a dramatic bleeding reduction, and that guidelines now recommend LAAC as an early first-line alternative to NOACs at high HAS-BLED. LAAO did not meet noninferiority; medical therapy including anticoagulation was better. The comparator was also best medical care, not NOACs alone.

### 9. Colchicine in Acute Myocardial Infarction (CLEAR SYNERGY / OASIS 9)

*NEJM · February 2025*

**The breakthrough.** The largest trial of colchicine after MI, and it was NEUTRAL. 7,062 patients received colchicine or placebo as soon as possible after PCI (mean 1.6 h). Over a median 2.98 years, MACE — cardiovascular death, recurrent MI, stroke or ischemia-driven revascularization — occurred in 9.1% versus 9.3%, hazard ratio 0.99 (95% CI 0.85-1.16). Diarrhea was more common with colchicine (10.2% vs 6.6%).

**Clinical guideline impact.** This is genuinely contested territory and worth understanding as a controversy rather than a conclusion. COLCOT (4,745 patients, recent ACS) and LoDoCo2 (5,522 patients, stable CAD) both found colchicine significantly reduced MACE. CLEAR SYNERGY is larger than either and found nothing. Investigators on both sides disagree about whether it is an outlier or the definitive answer.

**Practical implications.** Colchicine after MI is not a settled routine addition to discharge medications. Two positive trials and one larger neutral trial mean the honest position is uncertainty — and a resident should know the disagreement exists rather than learning one side of it.

[NEJM - Colchicine in Acute Myocardial Infarction](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2405922)

> **Citation check — ⚠ replaced — cited trial does not exist and the finding is contested.** There is no 'COLCOT-2' trial. The original said a large NEJM outcomes trial 'confirmed' that colchicine significantly reduces recurrent ischemic events and that guidelines elevated it to a routine recommendation. The large 2025 NEJM trial is CLEAR SYNERGY (OASIS 9) and it was NEUTRAL: HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.85-1.16) in 7,062 patients. COLCOT and LoDoCo2 were positive, so the entry now presents the genuine controversy rather than either side of it.

### 10. Intravenous Tenecteplase before Thrombectomy in Stroke (BRIDGE-TNK)

*NEJM · July 2025*

**The breakthrough.** Patients with acute ischemic stroke from large-vessel occlusion presenting within 4.5 hours were randomised to IV tenecteplase followed by endovascular thrombectomy, or to thrombectomy ALONE. Functional independence (mRS 0-2) at 90 days was 52.9% versus 44.1%, relative risk 1.18 (95% CI 1.01-1.39), P = 0.04 — the first randomised trial to show that thrombolytic bridging before thrombectomy beats going straight to thrombectomy.

**Clinical guideline impact.** Reverses the direction set by DIRECT-MT, MR CLEAN-NO IV and SWIFT DIRECT, which had all suggested bridging with alteplase was unnecessary. Tenecteplase is a genetically modified variant of alteplase, and it appears the agent matters.

**Practical implications.** Supports giving tenecteplase before transfer for thrombectomy rather than skipping thrombolysis. Weigh the safety signal: symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage at 48 h was 8.5% versus 6.7%, and 90-day mortality 22.3% versus 19.9% — both numerically higher with tenecteplase. Single bolus rather than an infusion pump is a real operational advantage during transfer.

[NEJM - BRIDGE-TNK trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2503867)

> **Citation check — ⚠ corrected — wrong trial name and wrong comparator.** There is no 'TIMELESS-2' trial. The real 2025 NEJM trial is BRIDGE-TNK (NEJMoa2503867), and the comparator matters: it compared tenecteplase PLUS thrombectomy against THROMBECTOMY ALONE, not tenecteplase against alteplase. The original's conclusion — that tenecteplase is preferred and the single bolus simplifies transfer — is defensible, but it does not follow from this trial in the way stated. Added the sICH and mortality numbers, which were both numerically higher with tenecteplase and were omitted entirely.
