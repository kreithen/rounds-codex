# "How Rounds Codex is made" — AI-assistance statement for the About page

Rewritten 2026-08-09. Supersedes the first draft, which was written before the attribution decision
and read defensively.

**Wording is Dr. Kreithen's to decide.** This is public copy about how his product is made and how
his review works — drafted, not decided.

---

## Recommended — for the About page

> ### How Rounds Codex is made
>
> **Every clinical statement here was written or reviewed by a practising clinician.** They are
> named above rather than described, because you should be able to see who stands behind it.
>
> **We use AI too, and we would rather say so than have you find out.** The illustrations are
> AI-generated from specifications we write, then checked page by page. Much of the question bank
> was drafted from our own condition modules and then reviewed and corrected. The app itself was
> built with AI engineering tools.
>
> That is what lets a small clinical team offer this much material. It does not replace the review —
> it makes the review the work.
>
> **Found something wrong?** Tell us. It reaches the clinician responsible, and it gets fixed.

**~110 words.** Sits under Clinical Advisors, above Share.

### Why it is written this way

- **Leads with the human claim, not the AI one.** The reader's question is "can I trust this on a
  ward", not "what tools did you use". Answer that first, and the AI paragraph reads as candour
  rather than confession.
- **"We would rather say so than have you find out"** does the tactful work you asked for. It
  concedes the awkwardness openly, which is disarming, and it is true — 950 gallery pages are
  visibly AI-illustrated to anyone paying attention.
- **Specific about which parts.** Vagueness is what invites *"so is the medical content
  AI-written?"*. Naming illustrations, question bank and engineering answers that before it is asked.
- **"It makes the review the work"** is the sentence that reframes it — AI as the thing that creates
  the reviewing burden, not the thing that removes it. More honest than claiming AI was incidental,
  and more impressive.
- **Ends on accountability, not on AI.** The last thing a reader takes away should be that a named
  clinician answers for errors.
- **No hedging.** No "some", "certain", "may have been". Hedges here read as evasion.

## Alternate A — plainer, if the recommended version is too conversational

> ### How Rounds Codex is made
>
> Rounds Codex is created and clinically reviewed by the practising clinicians named above, and
> produced with AI assistance.
>
> **Clinicians** write and review the clinical content, and no clinical statement reaches the app
> without one reading it.
>
> **AI** generates the illustrations from specifications we write, drafts question material from our
> own condition modules for review, and helped build the app.
>
> We are specific about which is which because the distinction matters more than sounding impressive.
> If you find an error, tell us — it reaches the clinician responsible.

**~90 words.** More formal. Better suited to a website About page or a press kit than a phone screen.

## Alternate B — one line, where space is tight

> Created and clinically reviewed by practising clinicians. Illustrated and drafted with AI
> assistance. Nothing clinical reaches you unread.

**~20 words.** For the App Store description, a footer, or the marketing site.

## Two decisions before this ships

**1. One sentence depends on an answer only you have.** The recommended draft says the question bank
was *"drafted from our own condition modules"*, which implies the modules are yours. That is Q1 in
`legal/provenance-for-counsel.md` — the repo shows the quizzes were generated *from* that text and
shows nothing about who wrote the text. If you wrote the modules, the line is right as it stands. If
they were AI-drafted and you edited them, change it to *"drafted from our condition modules"* and add
the modules to the AI paragraph. **The public statement and the copyright filing must not disagree.**

**2. Where else it appears.** The in-app statement and the App Store description contradicting each
other would be worse than either alone. Alternate B exists for that. Not required by Apple;
consistency is.

## What I would not say

- **"AI-powered"** — it is not a feature of the product, it is how the product was made. The other
  way round invites the reader to wonder what the AI is doing to them while they read.
- **"AI-assisted, physician-approved"** — *approved* is stronger and vaguer than *reviewed*, and it
  implies a formal sign-off process that would then have to exist.
- **Any number.** "90% human-reviewed", "over 1,800 reviewed questions" — a figure turns a statement
  of principle into a claim someone can audit. The principle is the stronger version.
- **An apology.** No "although", "despite", "still". AI assistance is how the thing got made and
  there is nothing to apologise for. The review is the promise.

## Note on attribution, carried over

Both drafts say **team of clinicians** rather than naming one person, per the 2026-08-09 decision.
Two things to keep straight as the group grows:

- **"Created by" and "reviewed by" are different claims.** With two clinicians listed today, if one
  authored and the other reviewed, "created by a team" attributes authorship to both. *"Created and
  clinically reviewed by practising clinicians"* covers both without assigning either, and stays true
  at any size — which is why Alternates A and B use it.
- **The app already matches.** Dr. Kreithen's advisor bio read "Writes and reviews the clinical
  content"; since v85 it reads "Leads the clinical content and review". His **Founder & Clinical
  Lead** role line is unchanged, because that is an accurate individual attribution and is not the
  thing being retired.
