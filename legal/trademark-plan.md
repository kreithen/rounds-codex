# Trademark — recommendation and filing plan

Written 2026-08-05. **I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.** It is a researched
starting point so the conversation with an attorney is short and the decisions are already framed.
USPTO fees and system names change — verify every number against uspto.gov before filing.

## Recommendation: yes, and the reason is specific to this project

Not because trademarks are generally a good idea, but because **the switching cost of the name is
already enormous and about to get worse.**

- **The name is burned into the artwork.** All 950 illustration pages carry
  `© 2026 Rounds Codex, Inc.` and the logo lockup in the footer. A forced rename means re-rendering
  every page — the same production pipeline that currently takes multiple rounds to get a row of
  dots right.
- `roundscodex.com`, the Zoho mailboxes, the Supabase project and the incorporated entity are all
  named for it.
- **The App Store listing makes the name public and permanently searchable**, and the app name is
  the strongest search-ranking field. Changing it later costs ranking as well as money.

That combination is the argument. A name this expensive to change is a name worth clearing and
protecting *before* launch, not after.

## What to file, in priority order

**1. `ROUNDS CODEX` as a standard character mark — file this first.**

A standard character mark claims the *words*, in any font, size, colour or styling. It is the
broader and more useful right: it stops someone using the name in a different typeface, which a
logo registration alone does not.

**2. The logo (design mark) — second, and optional at first.**

The pulse-circle mark plus wordmark. Protects that specific visual. Worth having eventually;
worth less than the words if you only file one. Note the logo is also **automatically protected by
copyright** as an original design, which the word mark is not — so filing the design mark is less
urgent than it feels.

### Is "Rounds Codex" a strong mark?

Reasonably, yes. Trademark strength runs generic → descriptive → suggestive → arbitrary → fanciful.
"Rounds" (ward rounds) and "Codex" (a bound manuscript) together *suggest* medical reference without
describing it — that puts it in **suggestive** territory, which is registrable on the Principal
Register without having to prove acquired distinctiveness. Much better placed than a descriptive
name like "Medical Study App" would be.

**The real risk is crowding, not weakness.** "Codex" is a heavily used term in software — including
a well-known AI product — and "Rounds" appears in medical apps. Different goods and classes usually
mean coexistence is fine, but *usually* is doing work in that sentence. **This is the single best
reason to pay an attorney for a clearance search** rather than filing on your own judgement.

## Filing basis: intent-to-use, almost certainly

This is the detail most people get wrong, and it matters here.

| basis | what it requires | fits you? |
|---|---|---|
| **§1(a) use in commerce** | the mark is *already* used selling goods/services across state lines, with a specimen to prove it | **No** — the app is unlaunched and the site is `noindex` |
| **§1(b) intent to use** | a genuine intent to use it; no specimen yet | **Yes** |

**Filing §1(b) now locks in a priority date before the App Store listing goes public.** If someone
else files a similar mark next month, your filing date is what decides who wins. After launch you
file a **Statement of Use** with a specimen (an App Store screenshot showing the mark on the listing
usually works) and the registration issues.

There is an extra fee for the Statement of Use, and extension requests if launch slips — budget for
it rather than being surprised.

## Classes — this is where the cost is

Fees are **per class**, so class choice is the main cost lever.

| class | covers | for you |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | downloadable software | **the iOS app — file this** |
| **41** | education and training services | **the educational content — file this** |
| 42 | SaaS / non-downloadable software | the website, if you want it covered separately |

Two classes is the sensible starting point. Three is defensible; each one adds a full application
fee.

### Reading the result honestly

This is the crowding risk, now concrete instead of theoretical. It is **not** a reason to change the
name, and not a reason to panic. The legal test is not "does CODEX exist" — it is whether
**ROUNDS CODEX as a whole**, in sound, appearance and commercial impression, is likely to confuse
buyers *given related goods*. A distinctive first word sometimes carries a mark past an existing
second word and sometimes does not, and that judgement is the thing an attorney is actually for.

**If class 9 turns out to be genuinely blocked**, the fallbacks in rough order: file **class 41**
(education services) alone; narrow the class 9 goods wording to medical-education software
specifically rather than software at large; or rely on the composite word-plus-design mark. Do not
abandon the name on one search result.

### What this suggests about filing

**Class 041 looks the friendlier of the two.** A dozen medical-education ROUNDS composites already
coexist there, and the CODEX marks in 041 are for unrelated subject matter (1099 business formation,
personal development, spirituality, legal process serving).

**Class 009 is the crowded one.** OpenAI holds three CODEX-family filings there and bioMérieux has
an issued registration. Nothing about that is fatal — the goods are language-model developer tools
and clinical diagnostics, not medical-student study material — but it is where an examiner is most
likely to push back.

Two practical consequences:

1. **Consider filing class 041 first**, or filing both with class 9 goods narrowed hard to
   *medical-education* software rather than software generally. Narrow goods reduce the overlap an
   examiner has to weigh.
2. **Separate the legal question from the commercial one.** OpenAI is a well-resourced owner with a
   CODEX family in class 9. A mark can be legally distinguishable and still attract an opposition
   that costs real money to answer. That is a business judgement about appetite, not a question of
   who is right, and it is worth raising with the attorney explicitly.

**Net read: this is a materially better position than "653 results" implied.** Nothing blocks the
name, the coexistence evidence is strong and quantified, and the candidates are down to about ten
identifiable marks with serial numbers.

## Steps on uspto.gov

1. **Search first — free, and do it before spending anything.** USPTO's trademark search is at
   **tmsearch.uspto.gov**. Search `ROUNDS CODEX`, then `roundscodex`, then `CODEX` alone in classes
   9, 41 and 42. **Filter to Live and to those classes** — an unfiltered search returns hundreds of
   irrelevant hits, as the 2026-08-05 run showed. You are looking for a *likelihood of confusion*,
   which is broader than an identical match — similar sound, appearance or meaning for related goods
   all count.
2. **Pick your goods/services wording from the USPTO ID Manual** rather than writing your own. Free-
   form descriptions attract extra fees under the current fee structure and are a common source of
   office actions. Search the manual for "downloadable software for medical education" and similar.
3. **File.** USPTO has been moving filings from TEAS to **Trademark Center** — start at
   uspto.gov/trademarks and follow whichever the site presents. You will need: owner name and entity
   type (Rounds Codex, Inc., a corporation, plus state of incorporation), a domicile address, the
   mark, the class(es), the goods/services wording, **filing basis 1(b)**, an email, and a signature.
4. **Docket the deadlines.** After filing you get a serial number; check status in TSDR. An office
   action has a **hard response deadline** and missing it abandons the application. This is the most
   common way a self-filed application dies.
5. **After launch: file the Statement of Use** with a specimen showing the mark used in commerce.

**Use ™ today — it is free and needs no registration.** It signals a claimed mark. **® is illegal
to use before registration issues**, so do not put it in the app, the listing or the artwork yet.
Worth checking the artwork on that point: at least one gallery (`nephrolithiasis`) arrived with a ™
variant in its logo lockup and it was treated as a defect. ™ is fine; ® is not, yet.

## Do you need an attorney?

**Not legally.** US-domiciled applicants may file themselves. (Foreign-domiciled applicants must use
a US attorney — not your situation.)

**But I would here**, for one reason: the clearance search. Filing is a form; deciding whether
"Codex" in class 9 is going to draw an opposition from an existing registrant is judgement. Flat-fee
trademark filings are commonly quoted in the several-hundred-to-low-four-figures range plus the
government fees, and a clearance opinion is the part you are actually buying.

A reasonable middle path: **run the free search yourself first.** If `ROUNDS CODEX` comes back clean
and nothing similar sits in classes 9/41, self-filing a standard character mark is a defensible
call. If the search turns up anything close, that is your answer about the attorney.

## Trademark is not copyright — you need both, for different things

| | protects | status |
|---|---|---|
| **Trademark** | the *name* and *logo* — stops others trading under it | not filed |
| **Copyright** | the *content* — the 950 illustrations, 1,820 questions, 181 condition texts | guide written, not filed |

**The copyright is arguably the more valuable of the two.** The 950 original illustrations are the
asset nobody else has and the one most likely to be copied outright. Registration is not required
for copyright to exist, but it is required to sue and it unlocks statutory damages — which is the
difference between a cease-and-desist that works and one that does not. The guide is already at
`legal/Rounds-Codex-Copyright-Registration-Guide.pdf` and registration is on the launch checklist.

If you only do one thing before launch, do the **free trademark search** — because it is the one
that could change the name, and everything else gets more expensive after that decision is locked.
