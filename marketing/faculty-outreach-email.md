# Faculty outreach email — draft

**Written 2026-08-25, the day iOS 1.0 was approved.** Faculty is the underrated channel: everything
else is retail, and one nursing programme that recommends the app delivers a cohort at a time. The
free period is the natural moment to ask, because there is nothing to sell.

**Read the claims policy in `marketing-brief.md` before changing a word of this.** The two rules that
bite hardest here: attribution is **plural** ("designed and clinically reviewed by a team of
clinicians", never "written by a physician"), and **no claim about exam scores or pass rates** — not
even softened. This email is going to educators who will check.

**The physician is the medical gate.** This is outreach about clinical content; it goes out over his
name and with his approval, not on my judgement.

---

## The email

**Subject:** A free offline clinical reference for your students — 183 conditions, 1,020 original illustrations

---

Dear Professor {LastName},

I'm a practising physician and the founder of Rounds Codex, a study reference for nursing and medical
students. It went on the App Store this week and it is free, with no account, no advertising and no
data collection of any kind. I'm writing to ask whether it would be useful to your students, and to
say plainly that there is nothing to buy.

What it is: 183 conditions across 21 specialties, each rewritten for the reader's level — assessment
and interventions in Nursing mode, pathophysiology and workup in Medical mode, management and
guidelines in Resident mode. The same library serves a student from first year through residency.

It also carries 1,020 original full-page clinical illustrations across 102 galleries, a 10-question
quiz on every condition (1,840 in total, each with an explanation), 1,010 USMLE-style and 150
NCLEX-style practice items, 300 drug entries, ten clinical calculators, and 470 guideline summaries
across 25 specialties.

Three things that may matter to you more than the counts:

- **Every illustration is original.** They were specified and reviewed by the clinicians who designed
  the curriculum rather than licensed from a stock library.
- **It works entirely offline.** The whole library is on the device after installation — which
  matters on a hospital floor with poor reception.
- **It collects nothing.** No account, no analytics, no tracking. Bookmarks and quiz progress stay on
  the student's device. Apple's privacy label for it reads "Data Not Collected".

It is a study aid and it says so on first launch, before any content is reachable: not medical
advice, not a replacement for a supervising clinician, and anything done for a real patient must be
approved by an attending or preceptor.

If it looks useful, the App Store link is below and I'd be glad for you to pass it to your students.
If you'd rather look first, I'm happy to walk you through it, and I'd genuinely value your criticism
of the content — that is worth more to me than a download.

With thanks for your time,

**{Full name}, MD**
Founder & Clinical Lead, Rounds Codex
{email}
{App Store link}

## Notes on the draft

**Why it opens with "there is nothing to buy."** Faculty inboxes are full of vendors. Saying it in
the first paragraph is the fastest way to be read as a colleague rather than a sales approach, and it
is true for as long as the free period lasts. **If a paywall is ever added, this email cannot be
reused as written** — the sentence would become a bait-and-switch.

**Why the counts come second and the three bullets third.** The numbers establish that it is
substantial; the bullets are the reasons an educator would recommend it over a competitor. Originality,
offline and privacy are the three claims that are both true and rare.

**Why it asks for criticism.** It converts a cold email into a request for expertise, which is the one
thing academics reliably answer — and it is not a device: a programme director's read on the content
is genuinely more valuable than one installation.

**What is deliberately absent:** any score claim, any pass-rate claim, any endorsement language about
the NCLEX or USMLE, and any suggestion of clinical use. Each would be false, and this audience is
exactly the one that would notice.

**Personalise the first line or don't send it.** A named course, a rotation, a paper of theirs. Faculty
outreach at volume with a mail-merge reads as spam and burns the channel for the real approach later.
